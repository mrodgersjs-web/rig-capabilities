# Engagement (Shanee Moret)

Commenting, replies, DMs, subscriber work and live chat. Synthesis in RIG's words; numbers are creator claims; every claim carries a video ID.

## Reciprocal, manual commenting

Her golden rule: give engagement to get it; posting and then disappearing is the slow ramp. She works the first posts in her feed one by one for about 10 to 15 calendared minutes a day, and says about 99 percent of people will not do this by hand [aCUbwMrQ8GA].

- Comment craft: say what you actually think first, give the creator visible credit before adding a different angle, and add something that helps the poster rather than praise alone; end with a question tied to the post [aCUbwMrQ8GA].
- Comment placement: when the poster will not reply or serves a different audience, reply to an aligned commenter instead, then connect with a short note referencing their comment; their reply exposes you to their wider network [aCUbwMrQ8GA].
- Like sparingly inside threads: roughly 1 in 25 comments (2 to 3 per 100) so you do not flood people with notifications (creator claim) [aCUbwMrQ8GA].
- Speed is part of the tactic: she reports replies and a connection within about 3 to 11 minutes on live demos (creator claim) [aCUbwMrQ8GA].
- Baseline volume for someone who does none: about 5 comments a day [toTLWp4aOtI].
- Early in her own growth, daily commenting doubled as market research and relationship building, without pitching in replies [i05h7QYsQIc].
- Comment prompts double as content research: ask viewers to name what they want next, or to drop one item in chat [5Y_tekIdDmI, v3TS92mls0k].
- Bots get blocked; she names no engagement pods anywhere and explicitly rejects pod-style reciprocity in two videos [toTLWp4aOtI, PJqb2j7d9qQ, Cv7QGjxA974].
- Tagging people in a video is, in her telling, checked by LinkedIn against whether they engaged, which discourages random tagging (creator claim, unverified) [EJ0QFdEXjqA].

## DM rules

- Give first: 5 to 10 genuine touches before any ask, and one video's guest version is give 10 times, ask once. A favor-asking first line fails; senior people refuse connection requests because instant pitches ruined the norm [R-74V2EOWdE, 4kkzQTa9Y-c, Cv7QGjxA974].
- DM only people who already engaged with the topic: event-promotion post commenters get a relevant note about their comment and the event, and that relevance is what makes them say yes [g6zmV-Y90rs, ebVmLIPyri8, Cv7QGjxA974].
- Keyword flow: a keyword comment on the post, then a DM that continues it; she reports 8 of 10 such DMs registering for the offer (creator claim) [X8Mz2tpvN-8, Cv7QGjxA974].
- Never DM-pitch event attendees you have not talked to; asking strangers for coffee at volume (she cites about 20 asks a day landing on busy leaders) is the spam complaint she hears from nine-figure operators [LaHXbztI_wo, R-74V2EOWdE].
- Keep notes short. Book-length DMs are why nobody replies; introductions between two people always carry the why [JwnyrcJAdaQ, R-74V2EOWdE].
- Warm beats cold: fresh registrants and subscribers come before strangers, and her estimate is that 100 cold notes might yield 5 replies and 2 calls while a warm week yields far more (her guess, not a test) [DdJYKsAfWqE, R-74V2EOWdE].
- The historical note: her 2018 playbook was cold DM pitching at scale (about 100 a day by her later telling) and she disavowed it on 2024-02-04; everything after is inbound-first [i05h7QYsQIc, WCWDDPFnoKU].

## Subscribers, registrants and quiet readers

- Subscribers are prospects: follow back, thank, comment, converse, and invite them to a live; each send also grows followers [1kqCOtDeLRA].
- Weekly subscriber review: identify second- and third-degree subscribers and send a short personalized connection note that references their subscription, never a long pitch; thank first-degree subscribers with a question. She reports 9 of 10 such requests accepted (creator claim) [JwnyrcJAdaQ, 93Wgv8WAHw4].
- The attend-click list from event signups is the saved warm prospect roster; there is no live-viewer roster, which is why she prompts chat to self-identify [Ec4xbXY8sjs, g6zmV-Y90rs].
- Expect the most valuable people to never like or comment: decision makers read quietly because their activity is visible to staff and boards, so network health is judged on replies, emails and calls, not post engagement [BDW169gFDy0, Ave2bYi7XRg, 62b9R_HMLAY, Ec4xbXY8sjs].
- A public commenter signals comfort being seen with you and is, in her guest's telling, very likely to answer a DM [Ave2bYi7XRg].

## Live chat and event engagement

- Open-ended questions get nothing. Give the room a keyword and a belief they already hold, and frame the benefit as theirs (connecting with the other people answering) [WbWSMLOeGW0].
- Mid-live prompts like a numbered self-identification ("type 1 if this is your goal") surface who is watching, since the platform gives no viewer list [Ec4xbXY8sjs, WbWSMLOeGW0].
- Live comments are research and content ideas; audience questions fill the next session [qSt2LtOCNy8, C_oq55RbYaU].
- One instruction at a time: never stack two asks (comment then DM) in the same moment [WbWSMLOeGW0].
- Answer after the prepared outline rather than letting chat derail the talk; thank subscribers, no pod mechanics [XSIlE64OTek, PJqb2j7d9qQ].
- Collaboration etiquette: choose guests for trust overlap not follower count, make it zero work for them, never ask the guest to promote, and take the clip duty yourself [ebVmLIPyri8].

## Dated shifts

- Her Feb 2025 advice keeps a floor of 5 comments a day for beginners and adds bot-blocking as feed hygiene [toTLWp4aOtI].
- By her 2026 video the goal shifts again: stop optimizing for visible decision-maker comments and optimize for DMs and booked business, because the buyer's engagement is private by design [62b9R_HMLAY, bCRYL6aL3ec].
