# Hot buttons and post anatomy (Taki rules applied to RIG)

Sub-skill of lv-strategy. Taki Moore's structure sits UNDER Mike's voice, never over it. This file
governs the buyer map and the skeleton a post must have; lv-hooks owns hook variants and lv-draft
owns the prose.

## Hot Button Map (draw one per topic, 60 seconds)

Four quadrants for one buyer and one topic:

| | immediate (this quarter) | long term (12-36 months) |
|---|---|---|
| away from | frustrations | fears |
| toward | wants | aspirations |

- The hook comes from the immediate column. Marketing here is declarative: state the frustration,
  never ask about it.
- The post opens on the frustration, closes on the aspiration, and the payoff line is the want.
- The test: if the buyer reads the first line and thinks "that is talking to me", the map is right.

RIG example, buyer = consultancy owner, topic = client intake: the frustration is inquiries landing
in three shared inboxes with no named owner; the fear is a large engagement lost to a reply that sat
six days; the want is every inquiry owned with a four-hour reply even mid-flight; the aspiration is
intake that reads as a competitive advantage before the prospect calls.

## Four legs (every post)

1. **Hook.** First line, at most 12 words, carrying a specific claim, number or image.
2. **Stack.** Body in exactly one of three forms: story, list, or steps. Not a mixture. Beyond about
   five short paragraphs, drop a mini re-hook between sections.
3. **Payoff.** One insight, one sentence, screenshotable. Three payoffs means three posts.
4. **Invitation.** A call to action OR a call to engage. Never both.

Pre-publish test: remove the invitation. If the post is no longer useful on its own it is pitch, not
content, and the payoff needs rewriting.

Note on hook style: Clark's 2026 evidence says formula phrasing reads as machine-made, so the 12-word
ceiling is scaffolding; the surface must read like a person talking. Rank hook variants through Jev.

## Three failures to audit for

1. Talking about method before the problem is declared.
2. Facts only, no feelings: facts carry immediate pain, feelings carry future aspiration. A post
   needs at least one of each.
3. Making Mike the subject. The buyer is the hero; Mike is the guide who names the map.

## Anti-patterns (rewrite on sight)

- Beige listicles with no hook, no payoff and no invitation ("5 considerations for AI adoption").
- Multiple calls to action on one piece.
- Any post where Mike is the hero.
- Throat-clearing openers ("in today's fast-paced AI landscape") and the banned word list.
- Diagnostic marketing: "what is your biggest challenge?" as a hook. Declare instead.
- A first line with no Hot Button Map entry behind it.
- Comment-keyword bait ("comment X and I will send the thing"): the platform detects and demotes it.
  Lead magnets travel through the profile path instead, at most monthly.
