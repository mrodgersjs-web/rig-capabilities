# About, Featured, photo, banner, button (third-party synthesis)

Synthesis of the RIG sub-skill and the profile-optimizer pack (third-party; paraphrased here). One rule binds all of it: photo, banner and copy are one artifact, and assets come from the designated generator (Eden), never hand-authored by an agent [lg-profile].

## Photo

Professional or as close to it as Mike can get himself. Not a camera-roll crop from a social night or a ceremony; that reads instantly. Expression: friendly, slight smile, not stern; the photo is where eyes land first and people connect with people [about-and-featured]. Stated cost reference: about $50 for a professional headshot [about-and-featured].

## Banner

Free advertising space that thousands of profile visitors see a month. No empty cover and no LinkedIn stock art. Copy tailored to the prospect and the benefit they get rather than to Mike or RIG, plus a logo, kept light on text [about-and-featured]. RIG correction: the banner is a brand asset and the generator produces it [lg-profile].

## About

- Address the reader, not a CV. Worked examples of people like the reader beat a chronology [profile-audit].
- Keep it to a handful of sentences: what Mike does, proof, what the reader gets, one call to action. The source doctrine tightened from 5-10 sentences to 4-6 over time; the tighter figure is kept [outbound-and-pipeline].
- The third-party pack structures it as opening hook, shared struggle, method, authority, then a single call to action, written in full sentences with breathing room between blocks and no manual line wrapping [profile-optimizer]. Structure serves the reader; the sentences stay Mike's voice.
- A CV-shaped About fails the audit [profile-audit].

## Featured

- One to three items, each pointing at a next step: book a call, the newsletter or a lead magnet, a case study. Three at most [outbound-and-pipeline].
- Strongest trophies first: a strong post, a case study, a testimonial [profile-audit].
- The third-party pack caps Featured at two external links with short benefit-led titles and no subtitles; where the two disagree, the smaller set is the safer read until Mike asks for more [profile-optimizer, outbound-and-pipeline].
- Featured images work like thumbnails with value and proof baked in [outbound-and-pipeline].
- Track every link (tagged URLs) so lv-measure can see what the profile sends [outbound-and-pipeline].

## Button and toggles

- The profile button (Premium) points at the same next step as Featured [outbound-and-featured, outbound-and-pipeline].
- The followers-display toggle (show followers instead of connections) is a display change that reads as authority to some audiences and as vanity to a sophisticated buyer, so it is a Lane A/B decision, not a default [about-and-featured].

## Formatting rules for generated copy

- Full sentences; never split a sentence across lines just to hit a width. The platform wraps text itself [profile-optimizer].
- Short punchy sentences are good; chopping sentences in half is not [profile-optimizer].
- No em dashes, no invented metrics [viral-core, profile-optimizer].

## Visual briefs

When the generator needs a brief: banner at the platform's banner dimensions with the chosen headline and a short tagline plus one CTA element; a profile picture that survives a circular crop; Featured tiles whose title text is the focal point with a simple visual cue and no body text. Each brief must be self-contained and state the brand colours [profile-optimizer]. The brief goes to Eden; the agent does not author the asset [lg-profile].
