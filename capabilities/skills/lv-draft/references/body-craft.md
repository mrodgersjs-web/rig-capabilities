# Body craft: dwell, saves, readability, voice

Objective: dwell time and threads. Dwell outranks likes in the reported ordering and LinkedIn has
published dwell as a feed signal; saves are the strongest reported signal. [body-craft: creator and
primary/secondary labels preserved from the source ledger]

## Structure and rhythm

- One idea per post. One point per line. Heavy whitespace so the post occupies more vertical real
  estate and is read slower. [body-craft]
- Write for saves: frameworks, checklists, blueprints, answer keys, "things people will want to
  deploy when they have more time". RIG's real artifacts qualify: the gate list, the kill review, the
  levels ladder. [body-craft]
- Write for comments: end on a question or a disagreement and seed one debate line mid-post. Threaded
  replies and multi-person back-and-forth outrank isolated pairs. [body-craft: secondary + ours]
- Reply-with-question: answering a comment with a question starts another thread.
- Story density beats length; emotional and failure posts over-perform. Do not write a lot just to
  write a lot. [body-craft, lg-draft]
- No wall of text, no stock imagery, no machine cadence. The fix for copy that smells generated is
  more context about Mike, not better prompting. [body-craft]
- Personal beats corporate; slang is fine; robotic is fatal. [lg-draft]

## Readability floor (gate, not a vibe)

Aim for a sixth-to-seventh-grade reading level and check it with a readability tool rather than by
feel. The named failure mode is one long single-sentence paragraph packed with corporate speak.
Simple language absorbs better regardless of how smart the reader is. [post-architecture: whisper
re-pass 2026-09-26, creator: Brook Miles, vHYBK9aNFfA]

## Cut filler, not just long words

In a phrase like "digital transformation solutions" no word is doing work. When two near-synonyms
both appear ("productivity and efficiency"), pick one. Concrete test: if a competitor could paste the
sentence onto their own site unchanged, it is filler. [post-architecture, creator: Brook Miles,
vHYBK9aNFfA]

## Self-promotion ratio

The ratio is not fixed at 80/20; it flexes by business type (success stories read differently for a
nonprofit than for a consultancy). Some self-promotion is still required, because it reminds people
you are in business. The house 4:1 give rule governs until this account measures otherwise; the
contradiction is recorded and unresolved. [post-architecture, creator: Brook Miles, eOaq7-IrKvY]

## Voice pass (scrub before the draft leaves)

- Strip machine tells: rhetorical questions stacked for effect, "it is not X, it is Y" constructions,
  corporate jargon, vague-but-smart-sounding statements. [creator: McTighe]
- Banned words: game changer, changed everything, leverage, and the RIG house list: unlock, empower,
  synergy, disrupt, hustle, utilize, iterate, optimize. [creator: McTighe + house]
- Read-aloud test: if it does not sound like Mike, rewrite. His register is operator with receipts:
  not consultant-with-frameworks, not founder-with-vision. [hook_library mike_voice]
- Small imperfections read human; over-polished template posts wear readers out. [clark:content-system]
- Raise safe drafts about 20 percent on Mike's own posts: frame beliefs as personal boundaries, keep a
  villain but punch up, and place the softening caveat low so readers argue in the comments. Comments
  on other people's posts stay positive. [clark:content-system nbXzXfthKl0]

## AI boundaries while drafting

AI may research, edit, stress-test an argument, repurpose long-form material and run the interview
for a solo founder. AI may not take a post from nothing to a draft, and AI-written comments are
forbidden entirely. One-click machine rewriting of a human draft is named as a trust destroyer;
context-fed drafting from real source material is a different act. [clark:content-system 8nBIAUSIRnQ,
