# Timing and algorithm

Sub-skill of `lv-creator-will-mctighe`. Synthesis in RIG's words; bracketed IDs are source videos. Every algorithm statement here is the creator's reading of the platform, unverified, and every number is a creator claim.

## 1. The first hour is not the game

His most quoted timing claim is negative: he retires the 60-minute death rule (2025-09-27), says acceleration often comes after 90 minutes, and reports that the first 90 minutes are not strongly tied to how a post ends up doing [Ej7WFBI9NS4]. By December 2025 he goes further: posting at 8:00 versus 9:00 and any scheduling ritual did not move results next to content quality [pyPoIYDg_a0]. He also reports posts three weeks old resurfacing in the feed [Ej7WFBI9NS4].

Dated shift: the September 2025 video still worries about links and some timing details [Ej7WFBI9NS4]; the December 2025 video drops those worries and calls clock time a non-lever [pyPoIYDg_a0]. Believe the later one, and note the conflict with Tommy Clark's playbook, which keeps launch-day actions (rewriting a weak opening early, not deleting slow posts in the first hours because delayed spikes are normal) [Fvl_wMtcz24, s6KMjhznXqs]. McTighe's rule is stronger: no golden-hour checklist at all.

## 2. Outliers, not averages

He frames distribution as a lottery with heavy tails: he cites 90% of six-month views coming from the top 20% of posts, illustrates with one post at 9,000 views against four near 250, and says one strong post often carries 2 to 3 times the rest combined [pyPoIYDg_a0, UJvAtV63vk0]. His operational answer is a month-long test loop: post daily for a month, rank by impressions, hypothesize whether topic, format or story caused the winner, then repeat winners [Ej7WFBI9NS4].

## 3. Winner reuse on a clock (2026-01-09)

A post that beat your average is the highest-probability next post: new followers missed it, old ones forgot it, and only a small slice saw the first run [A9acfOArp98]. His five-step reuse: sort 6 months by impressions, keep a bank of posts above a personal view threshold (he uses 200,000), rank case-study reuse by signups not views, and when short on ideas scan 6 to 12 months for a post unused for a while [A9acfOArp98].

| Variant | Window he gives |
|---|---|
| Similar-but-new angle on a winner | within a week [A9acfOArp98] |
| Same topic or title, new visual | 3 to 4 weeks [A9acfOArp98] |
| Exact rerun | 2 to 3 months [A9acfOArp98] |

Unreconciled: the same video praises a one-month exact repost before saying wait 2 to 3 months [A9acfOArp98]. Use the longer window. The June 2026 claim that generic recycled content is down-ranked does not cancel this: his own line is that a rerun carrying proof is not the same as a generic listicle [5rLlzb6_vgA, A9acfOArp98].

## 4. Speed windows that do matter

- **News:** tie the niche news to the problem and publish inside 3 days; do not summarize and do not wait [5rLlzb6_vgA].
- **Video:** hook inside the first 2 seconds [5rLlzb6_vgA].
- **Batching:** 2 to 3 hours, usually a Sunday session; he schedules 3 to 7 posts a week and reviews quarterly [TTH-75XOgJE, UJvAtV63vk0].
- **Cadence:** he posted daily from February 2024 for the growth phase [UJvAtV63vk0], with a weekly mix of 4 reach, 2 stories, 1 conversion [UJvAtV63vk0], 1 to 2 conversion posts a week [0_8TCrdBPVM], 1 photo and about 2 videos a week [pyPoIYDg_a0], and 3 to 4 carousel or infographic posts a week at peak [Ej7WFBI9NS4]. A quarterly spectacle is the cap on big stunts [5rLlzb6_vgA].

## Applying it (RIG)

- No golden-hour ritual is added to lv-launch on this doctrine's authority. If `linkedin-analytics-tracker` shows a first-hour effect on Mike's account, Mike's measurement wins and the ritual is rebuilt from that.
- Winner reuse becomes a proposal to lv-plan, not an automatic repost: every rerun is a fresh Gate-D item and a fresh Jev check (`post.linkedin`), and Lane A content Mike deleted after approving is never reposted without a new yes.
- The 2-3 month exact-rerun window is the safe default given his own unreconciled guidance.
- Algorithm statements stay labeled unverified anywhere they surface in a brief or a post premise. Never present one as a platform fact.
