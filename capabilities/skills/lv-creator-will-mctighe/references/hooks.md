# Hooks

Sub-skill of `lv-creator-will-mctighe`. Synthesis in RIG's words; bracketed IDs are source videos. Every number is a creator claim. Skeletons only: fill every slot with a sourced receipt and never publish a placeholder.

## 1. The hook is the forced view

McTighe's signature move is hook research at scale: he reports reviewing more than 1,000 viral posts and concluding that in 99 of 100 cases the opening is why the post spread [rlbI4ztEBFU]. His definition widens the hook beyond the first line: everything the scroller is forced to see is the hook unit.

| Format | Forced-view unit (his breakdown) |
|---|---|
| Image or text post | graphic plus the first 3 lines [rlbI4ztEBFU] |
| Video | 3 caption lines, the spoken opening, and the first couple of frames [rlbI4ztEBFU] |
| Carousel | first 2 caption lines plus slide one [rlbI4ztEBFU] |
| Infographic | the graphic plus 3 lines [rlbI4ztEBFU] |
| Long video | thumbnail plus the opening footage [rlbI4ztEBFU] |

So the visual is at least half the hook work, and a caption written last cannot rescue a weak slide [rlbI4ztEBFU, pyPoIYDg_a0].

## 2. Order of operations

1. **Clarity first.** The reader must get who it is for and what changes; only then add a lever [rlbI4ztEBFU].
2. **Add exactly one lever to start.** Surprise, curiosity, fear, agreement, or disagreement. He layers a second lever only after the first lands [rlbI4ztEBFU].
3. **Put the receipts in line one.** Proof-heavy or the most contrarian line moves to the top [rlbI4ztEBFU].
4. **Match the lever to the unit.** The forced-view element must carry it, not the third paragraph [rlbI4ztEBFU, pyPoIYDg_a0].
5. **Test with five interrupts.** Draft five pattern interrupts, keep the one that creates a feeling in you; he treats gut selection over the set as the method [9CsBaaYOgGM, TTH-75XOgJE].

Note the dated conflict with Tommy Clark: Clark stacks 2 to 3 levers in lines 1-3 as of 2026 [UjDZLhuEpnQ], McTighe says one lever first and layer later (2025-12-25) [rlbI4ztEBFU]. Both agree the opening must read like a person, not a formula.

## 3. The eight hook types (slot skeletons)

He teaches eight recurring types, ranked below by how un-copyable they are. Names are ours where noted; the mechanics are his [rlbI4ztEBFU].

1. **Proof or receipt (his favorite, and the AI-resistant one).** "[Result I have evidence for], and here is the artifact:" The claim a model cannot invent, then the lesson [rlbI4ztEBFU, wVWXbYuoXYg].
2. **Credibility jacking.** "[Famous name] [event], [my read]." The name must be famous to the buyer specifically, and you repay the borrowed attention with your own insight; tag the person when it is fair [rlbI4ztEBFU, D_MY33bviuQ]. He reports a named-person post at roughly 1,000 likes versus 200 to 400 for his norm [D_MY33bviuQ].
3. **Unexplained contrast.** "[A] versus [B]" with no explanation in the first line; the body resolves it [rlbI4ztEBFU].
4. **Mild controversy.** "[Niche] is wrong about [belief]." Controversy is now normalized on the feed, so the bar is a real disagreement on a real problem, not heat [Ej7WFBI9NS4, EUueg8xudKk, rlbI4ztEBFU].
5. **Fear or cost.** "[N] things that hurt [outcome]" or the money version, "[amount] mistake" [rlbI4ztEBFU].
6. **Emotional agreement.** A line the network wants to be seen agreeing with. He notes LinkedIn is not anonymous, so public agreement is a status act; keep the emotional line named to the buyer or it attracts no buyers [rlbI4ztEBFU, pyPoIYDg_a0].
7. **Visual novelty.** The stop is the asset: a color the feed is not using, a caricature instead of stock, a side-by-side layout [rlbI4ztEBFU, D_MY33bviuQ, 3UcZFurI_yw].
8. **Value promise.** "[N] [things] in [time]" or a clean promise of an outcome. Useful but the weakest alone; proof hooks overrun it [rlbI4ztEBFU].

Supporting patterns from his other videos:

- **Authority:** "[credential], [N years], [short time]" or "[N] lessons from [N years] at [named company]" [Ej7WFBI9NS4].
- **Journey:** "I used to [wrong belief]" followed by the change; pair a lived story with a topic that already travels [wVWXbYuoXYg].
- **Transformation:** "from [before] to [after] in [time]" for case studies [0_8TCrdBPVM].
- **Objection:** "[high-status buyer] challenges [offer]", answered as a dialogue, not a lecture [0_8TCrdBPVM].
- **Status contrast:** "[what most do] versus [what a top performer does]" [TTH-75XOgJE].
- **Harsh truth list:** "[N] harsh truths of [niche]" with the strongest two points at the top [O1LeyZsrnls, D_MY33bviuQ].
- **News tie-in:** the event first, your read second; never a recap of the news [5rLlzb6_vgA].
- **Challenge open:** "In [N weeks], [system] runs [function]", with the money or stakes stated and the result unknown [F8IGilhJItA].

## 4. Openings he says to drop

Rhetorical-question openings, the "not X but Y" construction, jargon, and words like "game changer", "changed everything" and "leverage" are on his own red-flag list, as are generic tip lists with no proof behind them [TTH-75XOgJE, pyPoIYDg_a0]. A self-disqualifying line is fine: he treats a reader who rules themselves out as a win, because the profile and the post should repel non-buyers [Ql4gXPDhalk, O1LeyZsrnls].
