# Visuals (Shanee Moret)

Images, covers, selfie video, short-video specs, AI renders and live slides. Synthesis in RIG's words; numbers are creator claims; every claim carries a video ID.

## Headshot and profile imagery

- Her 7-minute headshot stack: shoot several phone photos against a plain wall in different outfits, have an AI tool act as a strategic branding advisor and pick the strongest, sharpen it in an enhancer (she names Remini and YouCam), then finish in Canva with focus blur (she sets blur around 30) and color adjustment; optionally swap the background and re-center [z1mFUQ9MFSo].
- The chosen image should embody your positioning, not just look nice; her own demo framed a client as an advisor in a specific industry [z1mFUQ9MFSo].
- Vary outfits so the picker has real choices; keep the face sharp and pull blur back until it looks natural; watch that background swaps keep the subject centered [z1mFUQ9MFSo].
- The banner is a proof surface: features, stage photos, logos, book status, and in her 2026 framing the same signals serve AI agents reading your profile; one video also puts the offer URL on the banner as a passive reminder [bCRYL6aL3ec, DitOkxbdSvo].
- Photo matched to the buyer's world: what reads right for a venture-facing buyer differs from what reads right for a family-facing one [6f3ZzY5-gUg, bm6_uMvLagQ].

## Newsletter covers and post thumbnails

- A face beats a logo on a newsletter cover; one good face photo can serve unlimited editions, and covers change only if weekly consistency survives it [1kqCOtDeLRA, vNNVBp_mM0g].
- Keep covers phone-readable: no date piles, no logos, no tiny type, no crowds; plain layouts or title-beside-face; she builds them in Canva [PJqb2j7d9qQ, XzbX4QE1l1o].
- A/B test header images: face-plus-title against an AI-made header, on your own data [yeV4VseTmPQ].
- Event graphics answer the filtering questions at a glance (who it is for, what they learn) [g6zmV-Y90rs]; event thumbnails in her early video are called out as often messy, so treat them as a craft item [LaHXbztI_wo].

## Selfie video production checklist

From her critique of a challenge cohort's selfie videos [EJ0QFdEXjqA, WbWSMLOeGW0]:

- Face natural window light (she prefers it over ring lights for glasses-wearers; a clamped key light is her paid pick) [EJ0QFdEXjqA, WbWSMLOeGW0].
- Camera at or slightly above eye level; show hand gestures early; watch headroom and forehead cropping; use a real background over a virtual one [EJ0QFdEXjqA].
- Audio is half the experience (creator claim); keep the mic clear of hand gestures with a boom arm; a visible mic correlated with her best lives, though she says the cause is unproven [EJ0QFdEXjqA, WbWSMLOeGW0].
- Front light against pale backgrounds; declutter and add depth to the frame [WbWSMLOeGW0].
- Unedited beats polished: the stumble or blemish can draw the most traction, and your delivery and accent are the non-duplicable part; imperfect daily video beats polished text posts [4kkzQTa9Y-c, WCWDDPFnoKU, xwCLAdZTJgE].
- Gear is the wrong early spend: no cameras, teleprompters or studios before you can hold a room; live reps build on-camera skill faster than equipment [YBYkNwplEmE, XSIlE64OTek].

## Short-form video specs

- Shoot vertical; about 45 to 90 seconds performs best and up to 2 minutes works; open on the point with no greeting; one specific actionable idea per clip; burn in captions; keep it unbranded with no logos or watermarks [YggvR0eUj6E, 0cQdGNRvatM].
- The post text mirrors the clip; the next piece in the series continues the same topic [YggvR0eUj6E].
- Tools she names: Captions, CapCut (mobile and desktop), Descript, and Opus or Descript to cut long recordings into shorts [YggvR0eUj6E, 8mwU4HPHgHM].
- The video tab is a search surface too: by 2025 shorts sit in search and on desktop, so an on-screen topic label matters; her viral-reel rule is one new action for one buyer against one 90-day goal [toTLWp4aOtI, 0cQdGNRvatM].
- Trends feature (mobile): record in-app or edit elsewhere and upload, short caption, post immediately since trend clips cannot be scheduled; her trend clip reportedly reached roughly 800,000 impressions in about a week (creator claim) [cY6L5HMglEQ].
- A prerecorded file can be played as a live through StreamYard, or attached to an event you already promoted; plain thumbnail or title-beside-face, and never a fake native upload. She teaches this on 2024-11-14 while also urging people to actually show up, and the conflict is unresolved in her own corpus [XzbX4QE1l1o, XSIlE64OTek].

## Live and webinar visuals

- Start on camera before bringing slides up so face-only clips can be cut later; move slides often (she says roughly every 30 seconds), hold the most important point for last, and trim the hesitant first seconds on the replay [WbWSMLOeGW0, rI4ZkkauCcU].
- Storyboard first, shoot only the scenes you need, and let AI assembly be good enough for LinkedIn even when it sits below a human editor [ebVmLIPyri8].
- Her 10/20/30 style rule for slide-led teaching: about 10 slides, about 20 minutes, large type around font size 30 [YggvR0eUj6E].
- Product businesses can put physical items on camera; the recording becomes a YouTube asset and clips [C_oq55RbYaU, rI4ZkkauCcU].

## AI images versus real photos (dated conflict)

Her undated 2026 tool test (video captured about 8 months before the 2026-09-28 scrape) found that a story-attached AI image can outperform a real photo for a small account, provided the render passes her essence test: plausible pose, your real hair, skin texture and expression, not a costume-swapped face. She builds it from a recent real headshot as the reference image, judges on likeness over polish, keeps each winning prompt in a fresh chat as a reusable prompt library, and downloads the file to upload natively rather than sharing generated links; she rated the better render about 87 percent of a real shoot (creator claim) [qPHqqOfZSEo].

This conflicts with Tommy Clark's 2026 rule that real photos of real people win, backed by his own roughly 27 percent impressions claim [szN0DF3Oqik, 2Yv8osegsKY]. Dates: Clark's measured claim is 2026; her test is 2026. Where they disagree, believe the dated measurement and Mike's own analytics; for Mike, an AI render of him or of Lane A cast members is a Gate-D image asset and the cast rules (Birch Tuesday-only, The Coach only from behind or in silhouette) still bind.
