# Substack, X and the owned list

These are the three channels that fail for carrier reasons, not writing reasons. Know the carrier before promising the channel.

## Substack: an owned-list asset, not a Tuesday amplifier

- There is no public Substack API. The delivery path is a browser extension on a machine that has to be awake, installed and signed in at the scheduled minute. A green "Connected" tile in the scheduler is an intention, not a delivery mechanism.
- Do not schedule into it until one issue lands and is read back at the publication. The gate refuses a Substack channel whose browser was not live at publish time.
- What it is for: a 30-90 day owned-list asset. A day-zero newsletter has zero subscribers, so it cannot amplify a post that is already hours old. Stand it up before the next post, not during one.
- The correct shape is a weekly digest: one email, the week's LinkedIn posts as sections with the LinkedIn link inline, plus one thing that exists only in the email so subscribing earns something. Never auto-syndicate individual posts; that is a firehose with no reason to open it.
- Publishing an article needs explicit choices (title, subtitle, whether it sends as an email). Ask for them; never guess.

## Newsletter curation (when the digest is a curated issue)

1. Score every candidate item against the newsletter's editorial promise in one line; discard anything that merely shares a topic.
2. Fill fixed sections by slot, ordering for reading momentum (strongest second, most useful last).
3. Write one to three sentences of commentary per item: why it matters to this reader, not what it says.
4. Check balance; no single source or topic dominates unless it is the theme.
5. Emit the issue skeleton with the cut list and the rejection reasons. The cut list is the point of view.

## X: the API carrier, never the browser

- The carrier is the scheduler's own social provider stack out to the linked handle. Not browser automation: the browser's active session on this machine is a 15-year-old's recruiting account, and any browser-driven X write would ship RIG content onto a minor's recruiting profile. Assert the linked handle before any X write and halt on mismatch.
- Write to 280 characters. The "I have X Premium" checkbox stays unticked until a human confirms paid status on the real handle, because ticking it asserts something unverified and guessing produces truncated posts. The verification click is Mike's, not an automation task.
- Shape: X rewards the claim stripped of its scaffolding, not a trimmed post. Pull the one load-bearing sentence, keep the number, and put the LinkedIn link in a second post or a quote-repost so the first stands as a thought. A one-post link-drop reads as syndication and is ignored.
- X is also an input channel: it earns value from trend-scouting whether or not it ever publishes. Do not measure it only by output.
- Connecting or re-consenting a platform is one human click and cannot be completed programmatically; reading an already-rendered account list is fine, clicking to re-link is not.

## Owned email

Highest intent, zero platform risk, and currently a template: no owned list exists in this lane yet. Until one is named, the channel does not count in a distribution plan. Never solve it by buying a list; an uploaded purchase list turns the sending domain into a spam complaint. When a list exists: adapt the post into a newsletter format, add a "reply to this email" CTA (replies are the email equivalent of comments), link to the LinkedIn post for social proof, and send within two hours of the post going live.
