# Timing and algorithm (Kasey Brown)

Hour-one behavior, cadence, week design, quiet seasons, and his algorithm readings. Every algorithm statement below is his own interpretation and is unverified.

## The first 60 to 90 minutes

- His named rule: reply to every comment in the first 60 to 90 minutes after publishing. Early engagement and reply activity are what he believes pushes distribution, and the thread is also where the DM pipeline starts. [hC7ZCVsG7qM, 4jnS31Fh5QE]
- Do not schedule a post you cannot stay for. He refuses to publish unless he is free for hour one, and some days he skips posting entirely for that reason. He still ran a scheduling tool earlier in 2025, so his operational answer is presence, not tooling. [uHgHT0fg3As, TcvoXYSHqe4]
- 15 minutes of commenting before publishing warms the account for the post that follows. [uHgHT0fg3As]
- His team is alerted at publish time to help manage incoming comments and DMs on big posts. [jcxbBuiW2vE]

## Cadence, dated

- 1 to 2 strong posts a week produced his early million-impression weeks (2025-03). [4jnS31Fh5QE]
- 2 to 3 posts a week (2025-06). [TcvoXYSHqe4]
- Six days a week for his first seven months produced almost no engagement (his own account of it, 2026-02); cutting from six a week to 3 or 4 raised engagement (2025-07). [EfHijF0P6bo, B18w0jPdXiI]
- His standing number is 3 to 4 posts a week; he publicly disagrees with a guest who teaches daily posting 5-7 days a week as a minimum, arguing that 3 a week for a year beats daily for two months and burnout. [eyfNP4ipgrI, iVnkmeVj9ao, 2t-EBcS43Lw]
- Launch weeks are different: 5 to 6 posts a week through a 41-day launch window. [dI8ZqDwPBNY]
- Guest counterpoint, dated 2025-10: a large creator posts 2 to 3 times a day with 4-6 hours between same-day posts and calls 2 to 3 a week too aggressive for his own growth. [DXqN9z7HK-w]

## Week and day design

- Weekly slots: Monday contrarian for fresh eyes, Wednesday framework or behind-the-scenes, Friday industry insight or relationship because his buyers read over the weekend; plan Sunday from the week's real calendar, notes and mistakes. [2t-EBcS43Lw, B18w0jPdXiI]
- Executive targeting: post at commute time (his case: Monday and Friday at 6:30 a.m.) when the named 50 decision makers are reading. [-1fJ7U684Hs]
- Guest practice: a fixed daily slot (10 a.m.) plus a monthly batching day for a 20-day queue. [eyfNP4ipgrI]
- Quiet seasons are low-competition windows: his December challenge runs in the least competitive weeks of the year, and one participant got a business started from a single post in that window. [lX3xNg2Ekw4]
- Newsjacking timing: he skips weekend posting and enters a big story Monday or Tuesday with the untaken angle, because the first angle gets the reach. [LvA5pocucUM]
- Counter-evidence on holiday launches, dated 2026-03: his holiday-window launch collected six figures while on break, so the conventional never-launch-over-holidays advice failed in his data. [dI8ZqDwPBNY]

## Algorithm claims (creator readings, unverified)

- Distribution rewards dwell: the see-more click and long read time are the signals he believes matter most, with sharing second and likes or comments weaker; his reasoning is that a platform selling ads wants posts that keep people on-platform. [LvA5pocucUM, 4jnS31Fh5QE, m9VFFCW_qZs]
- Early comment activity and replying amplify reach. [hC7ZCVsG7qM, uHgHT0fg3As]
- Only about 10 percent of followers see any given post, which is why repetition and consistency compound. [bCxD79Wl6ec, vBxw5q522pY]
- The 2026 "reach is collapsing" panic is fear-marketing in his reading: LinkedIn never announced the alleged overhaul, and his numbers grew during the claimed crisis because his approach does not depend on algorithm hacks. He argues the content game (volume, pods, timing tricks) eventually gets punished by any change while positioning is immune. [iVnkmeVj9ao]
- Connecting triggers a favorable moment: when someone accepts, LinkedIn surfaces one of your better posts to them, so he DMs new connections within 48 hours. [NjyEXaX8nQU]
- Supply and demand: roughly 1 percent of users create, so creators get disproportionate distribution now; he treats this as a closing window and says he would switch platforms if the ratio flipped. [hkIHWVjv4yk, pNhM9BttZLQ, DXqN9z7HK-w]

## Applying it (RIG)

Never schedule a post Mike cannot sit with for 90 minutes; publish by hand, then run the reply loop. Plan the week as three slots and fill them from real events, not a blank page. Algorithm readings stay labeled as hypotheses in any report and get tested against `linkedin-analytics-tracker` numbers; where this doctrine and Tommy Clark's differ (Clark boosts underperforming posts with paid spend), the RIG ad budget and Gate-D decide, not the doctrine.
