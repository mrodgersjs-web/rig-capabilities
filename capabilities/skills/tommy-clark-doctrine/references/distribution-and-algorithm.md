# Distribution and the algorithm

Sub-skill of `tommy-clark-doctrine`. Every algorithm statement here is Clark's dated, unverified claim. He says anyone who claims to fully understand the algorithm is lying [s6KMjhznXqs]. Test against Mike's analytics.

## 1. How he describes the feed (Sept 2026)

- **From network to interest graph:** distribution increasingly follows topic interest rather than who follows you, echoing Meta's shift; accounts may be sorted into topics. [s6KMjhznXqs, BMfXeo02bvo]
- **Spiky and late:** a post can look dead for 12 hours and then take off; per-post variance is high. [s6KMjhznXqs]
- **Longer shelf life:** the feed resurfaces posts one to four weeks old, so old posts must stay clean. [_tON8MpkmS0, N2b0QVoCEPg, SL8yfxUVFKE]
- **Organic reach is down:** he cites mid-size accounts averaging about 2,500 impressions and a study showing about a 10% drop that he himself calls shaky. [BMfXeo02bvo, s6KMjhznXqs, tYd_MnpXU90]
- **Barbell:** the feed favors very high-effort or very scrappy posts; the polished middle invites AI suspicion. [s6KMjhznXqs]

## 3. After you post (his checklist, updated to 2026)

1. Answer every comment in the first 30-60 minutes, ideally with a question that extends the thread.
2. Ask teammates for real comments; mobilize the wider network only for big moments (launch, fundraise).
3. Watch the early numbers. Rewrite a lagging opening if needed, but do not delete in the first hours.
4. Comment on 10-15 posts from buyers and the voices they follow.
5. Send the day's connection requests.
6. Review profile viewers for warm signals.
7. At 24 hours, write down one to three learnings.
8. Bank the winner and schedule a rerun.

## 4. Low-engagement diagnosis (check in this order)

1. Opening: two or three levers in lines 1-3?
2. Media: a real photo with people in it?
3. Story: is each take paired with one?
4. Intensity: turned up about 20%?
5. Timing: a weekday mid-morning?
6. Audience ceiling: engineering, procurement and IT buyers read but rarely react; founder, marketing and sales audiences react. Set the benchmark before blaming the content.
7. Topic reach: when niche posts stall, widen within the buyer's world (not empty controversy).
8. Engagers: are the people reacting actually buyers?

- Judge a post against the account's own baseline; a 100-like post on a 10-20-like baseline is the signal to study. [0EA0n1Ka41k, E0uu_GLIkw0]
- He warns that chasing likes is a trap; the target is customers. [zMzPmTETIyU]

## 5. Paid distribution: thought-leader ads (2025-26)

- Let the post run organically for one to two weeks. Boost proven winners and ICP-relevant posts that under-delivered (he names roughly 17-31 likes). Start at $20-30 a day (about $900-1,000 a month).
- Before boosting, edit a PS line with a link into the post; boosted posts run only on awareness or engagement objectives, so the PS is the only call to action.
- Target with an uploaded company list (for example from Apollo), which he finds more accurate than native filters. Feed the paid engagers back into outreach, and mine the ad demographics and company report for target accounts.
- He says boosted posts keep their organic engagement and look native.

## 6. Manufactured peaks

Steady posting grows slowly and linearly. Launches, fundraises and real-world stunts create spikes that own the feed for a day; he calls the physical-to-digital version "IRL to URL." Plan at least one peak on top of the cadence. [1je0kCMMstg, 29hBCF7N7Jg, Xt179qkyqQU]
