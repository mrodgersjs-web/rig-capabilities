# Channel order, gates and route-back

## The order, cheapest reach first

`LinkedIn Groups -> X -> owned email -> Substack (weekly, never per post) -> Reddit (comments daily per ramp, posts only once earned) -> trade publications (2-4 week lead) -> podcasts (relationship, evergreen)`

Run left to right and stop when the budget of attention is out. Two corrections that people get wrong:

1. **A trade publication goes first, not last, when it claims first run.** Read `first_run_required` off the queue before drafting anything. If two pieces share a thesis, the outlet version is written and submitted before the LinkedIn version exists.
2. **Reddit is not an amplifier at all.** It is a 14-day account-building obligation that precedes the right to ever post there. Treating it as a same-day distribution channel is how an account gets banned.

## Four gates (each is a gate, not advice)

1. **`adapt_not_copy`.** Two channels receiving near-identical text inside 48 hours is a silent delivery failure: the carrier rejects duplicates and a manual retry 33 hours later landed inside LinkedIn's own suppression window. RED at 0.90 similarity or above, across any pair or against the source post.
2. **`route_back_compliance`.** The route-back is per channel, never uniform (table below). A uniform "link back to my LinkedIn post" is a permanent ban on one channel and a pitch rejection on another.
3. **`pub_target_verified`.** A pitch goes to a route that exists today: the contact must have been read off the outlet's own page within 120 days, the route type must be editorial or contributed (not advertising), and the outlet's AI-assistance policy must be known. Anything else is refused.
4. **Attribution.** Amplification attempts are inputs; the only output is a LinkedIn or site metric that moved and can be attributed. Every route-back link carries a distinct `utm_source` that is already in the site's closed measurement allowlist. An unreviewed value collapses into "other", which makes the channel unmeasurable and is functionally identical to not having run it.

## The route-back map

| Channel | Where the LinkedIn or site reference goes | Why there |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn Groups | last line of the post body | renders the preview card carrying the image |
| Reddit | profile bio (standing) plus a comment reply only when someone asks | body links are a permanent-ban clause in several target subs; a comment link reads as an answer |
| X | in-post link, or a quote-repost pointing at the thought | preview-card channel; a link is native behaviour |
| Substack | in body and footer, freely | owned ground |
| Owned email | in body plus a "join the conversation" CTA | social proof is the point of the link |
| Trade publications | nowhere in the piece; bio line only | first-run exclusivity and vendor links get pitches rejected |

## Hard stops

- Nothing sends from this stage: every group post, Reddit post, X post, newsletter issue and pitch email is a batch-level Gate-D approval naming recipients and exact text.
- Assert the signed-in identity before every outward action and halt on mismatch. One browser holds Mike's and a client's session at once.
- Lane C content is out of scope at every approval level: no cross-distribution of a minor's content, no offer, no CTA, no funnel, no auto-schedule.
- No fabricated engagement anywhere in this stage; since the feed began scoring engagement as percentile buckets, fake engagement re-classifies the real audience as bots.
- Every number carries its evidence class; reader-reach figures from how-to content are `[claimed]` and never reach copy as fact.
