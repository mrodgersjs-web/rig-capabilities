# Strategy argument (planted GREEN fixture: Tesla's early strategy as told in CLAIM(y1-5nxulR68))
Context and category: Cars. Customers shopping for an electric car compare it with other cars they could buy; in the late 2000s the electric-car category meant small, slow, expensive city runarounds sold on economy (e.g. the G-Wiz).
Status quo and why it sucks: Electric cars were expensive because the technology was new, and undesirable because they were small, slow and unattractive. Nobody rational pays a premium for an unexciting car, so the category was not moving.
Unique belief: Electric cars are not about economy, because they are expensive anyway. Electric drivetrains make smooth, very fast cars, so the first electric cars should be performance and luxury cars that people desire. Every competitor believed the future of EVs was economy and cheapness and would have told Tesla to go ahead with its "stupid idea".
What we will do: Launch a supercar first (the Roadster), then a high-performance luxury sedan (the Model S), and use the margin and attention to fund progressively more affordable models.
Value to customers: Electric cars become fun, desirable status objects, which no other EV maker offers.
How we must change / trade-offs: No small economy city car at launch; no competing on price in the first products.
Strategy statement: We are the only EV manufacturer who makes a supercar our first car, so customers get luxury, performance and status from an electric car.
Execution: Delivery: build the Roadster on a sports-car platform, then the Model S. Communication: sell performance and desire to early adopters who will pay a lot, not economy.
