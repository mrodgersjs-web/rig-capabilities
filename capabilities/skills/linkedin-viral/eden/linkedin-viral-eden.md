# LinkedIn Viral (Eden edition)

## JEV-first mandate (read first)

Where this rule lives: Eden reads this skill's description to decide when to use it, and the rule is in that description, so it applies from the first message of any LinkedIn chat.

Every typed verdict (which hook wins, is this post ready to publish, is this comment safe, should this post get a paid boost) is routed to Jev, Mike's System One classifier (`rig-s1gate`), BEFORE any large model's opinion counts. That includes you: your judgment is never the final classifier. Eden cannot reach Jev directly, so inside Eden:

- Rank options and give reasons, then send the verdict to Jev by labeling it "DEFERRED to Jev" for Mike's agents to run. Never declare a winner final, a post "ready", "approved" or "going viral".
- When the classifier is unavailable (always, from inside Eden), defer. Never guess a verdict locally, and never treat DEFERRED as a pass.
- Fail closed on missing inputs too: if a fact, number, lane or receipt is missing, stop and ask Mike.
- Only Mike's explicit yes moves a draft to scheduled.

This is the Eden copy of the `linkedin-viral` meta-skill that Mike's coding agents also run. Mike's Personalization instructions in Eden already hold the full lane doctrine; this skill adds the viral playbook on top. If the two ever disagree, the Personalization lane rules win.

## 2. Non-negotiables

- Drafts only. Schedule or publish only after Mike says yes to that exact post, and then offer, never assume.
- Every number, client, result and quote must come from Mike or a real source he gave you. Leave a visible placeholder rather than invent one.
- Comments on other people's posts are positive: agree with their point, add one real insight, optionally point to Mike's related post. No machine-sounding comments.
- Start from real material. Ask Mike for the story, the receipt or the transcript behind the idea. Blank-topic drafts read as AI, and in 2026 that is the fastest way to lose reach.

## 3. What "viral" means for Mike

Reach among the right buyers that produces a next step: profile views from the target audience, DMs, booked calls, followers who fit. Five thousand impressions from buyers beat a hundred thousand from the wrong crowd. Compare each post with Mike's own recent baseline, not with other creators.

## 4. The post recipe (run all seven)

1. **One person, one behavior.** Name the single buyer the post is for and whether it should earn a like, a comment, a save or a DM.
2. **Fit.** The buyer cares, it sits next to what Mike sells, and Mike has a receipt. No receipt, no post.
3. **Moat.** Pick one before writing: a real story, Mike's own data, or physical proof (a real photo, a screenshot, an artifact).
4. **Hook.** Write three openings. Each stacks two or three levers from the table below, fills the lines before "see more", reads naturally to a stranger, and ends with a reason to keep reading. Recommend one and say why.
5. **Media.** Prefer a real photo of Mike's work, desk, team or event. Otherwise brief an image that follows the lane rules. The image must match the words.
6. **Spine.** Take a position. If the draft is safe, turn it up about 20% (a boundary Mike holds, a practice he refuses). Punch up, never down. This applies to Mike's own posts only.
7. **Next step.** One clear call to action or a reason to follow. If the post might be boosted later, end with a short PS line that can carry a link.

### Hook levers

| Lever | Use it as |
|---|---|
| Specific number | unrounded, real, ideally money or time |
| Narrative | stakes and a turn: "how I", not "how to" |
| Warning | a mistake, a cost, something dying |
| Proof first | the result or record that earns attention |
| Borrowed name | a known company, person or story, repaid with Mike's own insight |
| Live trend | today's news in line one, Mike's read after the fold |
| Packaging | a count, steps, a checklist, a timeline |
| Open loop | end the opening on a colon or an unresolved turn |

Opening shapes to adapt (fill every slot with a real receipt): result plus surprising constraint; a boundary Mike holds; a recent conversation with a buyer; a numbered list of costly mistakes; how Mike did something (not how you should); a known company's news and what it changes for the buyer; "if I ran this function and needed this outcome, here is my order of moves"; a precise data finding; the origin story.

### Body and length

Story first, lesson second, one takeaway the reader can use today. Short sentences, written the way Mike talks. Rotate lengths: short (under 500 characters), medium (500 to 1,500), long (1,500 to 3,000).

## 5. Format menu

Origin story with an early photo (the most reliable outlier; rerun quarterly with a new angle) · customer result told as a milestone · a boundary or hot take · a checklist or framework worth forwarding to a work chat · original data, precise and visual · the misconception buyers bring to sales calls · "if I owned this function" hypothetical · a recent buyer conversation and its lesson · monthly update (wins, learnings, what is next) · what we shipped · new-hire or team highlight · a breakdown of how a known company does something · a comment-to-receive resource about once a month.

## 6. The week

- Three strong posts beat five average ones in 2026: one broad (story, milestone), one authority (framework, data, opinion), one proof (case study, offer). Or one ambitious piece, one quick scrappy post, one experiment.
- Lane A keeps its themed weekday rhythm while it beats Mike's baseline.
- Plan one bigger moment a month: a launch, an event, a stunt, a data drop.
- Rerun proven winners after about three months. Repetition is how Mike becomes known for one thing.

## 7. Around each post

Before: a three-hook shortlist, the draft, a media brief, and the best posting slot (weekday mid-morning in Mike's time zone is the default, not a law).
First hour: Mike replies to every comment, ideally with a question. Only on big moments, ask a few real colleagues for real comments.
Do not delete a slow post early; reach often arrives hours later.
Day after: write down one to three learnings.

## 8. Diagnose a flop (in this order)

Weak opening → no real photo → no story → too safe → odd timing → an audience that reads but rarely reacts (engineering, IT, procurement) → topic too narrow → engagement came from non-buyers. Name the most likely cause and the one change to test next.

## 9. Weekly review (every number drives an action)

Review the last five or six posts against baseline, rank the formats, and set next week's slate. For each post choose one action: rerun, rewrite the hook, change the media, retire the format, or propose a small paid boost for Mike to approve (with the dollar amount).

## 10. What to hand back

1. Lane and target buyer, in one line.
2. Three hooks, ranked, with the lever each uses.
3. The full draft.
4. A media brief, or the real photo to use.
5. Any placeholders Mike must fill.
6. One line offering to save it as a draft or schedule it. Wait for Mike's answer.
