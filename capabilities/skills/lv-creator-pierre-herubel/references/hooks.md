# Hooks (Pierre Herubel)

The hook is the first two sentences, the part visible before the "see more" break, and he treats it as the single biggest lever on a post's reach [i-YDUfx84f8, Io7Uq-S8dpk, c_2g0KM7fqY].

## What a hook has to do

- Three jobs, stated repeatedly: attract the click, name the topic, and make the intended reader feel addressed [i-YDUfx84f8, Io7Uq-S8dpk, 2eovlLOHOU4, SmuGlt1CDKY].
- His three quality criteria are specific, simple, unique. Generic hooks are named as the second reason posts stall under a thousand impressions [SmuGlt1CDKY].
- Numbers in both lines add the specificity, and one hot-topic keyword of the industry adds the emotional pull (he distinguishes these from empty power words) [2eovlLOHOU4, i-YDUfx84f8].
- Never a deceptive hook. Deliver the promised answer first, at the top of the body, then develop it; a hook that earns the click and then bores keeps only a partial boost [Io7Uq-S8dpk, SmuGlt1CDKY, 2eovlLOHOU4].

## Two-layer hook

- Layer one is visual: the photo or infographic and the headline printed on it. Layer two is text: the first two lines. Both decide the click [mV1Ma2ZVgU8].
- The headline on the image sets the angle the reader holds while reading the whole post, so it must match the text hook rather than decorate it [CphfSIw7WV8, 0ZKNmQnMQ7k, i-YDUfx84f8].
- Over a long enough run of useful posts, your name and face become the hook, and the reader's expectation does the attracting [c_2g0KM7fqY, 4OVxO2j24sw].

## Hook families he names

- Negative hooks, problem-agitate-solution hooks, numbered lists, and a short dialogue format [BkdOFSfFPD4].
- A surprising fact opener, a direct address to the reader, or a problem stated then agitated [i-YDUfx84f8].
- Curiosity from a stated limit (an outcome you supposedly cannot choose) followed by the workaround [CphfSIw7WV8].
