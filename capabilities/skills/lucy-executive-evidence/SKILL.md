# Lucy executive evidence

Turn the employer's mandate into answers Mike can defend. This is a Mike-specific evidence workflow using Lucy's interview structure; it is not a claim that Lucy authored or approved the whole workflow.

Read [the evidence contract](references/evidence-contract.md) before handling claims. Read [the source map](references/source-map.md) when applying or quoting a Lucy method. Read [worked examples](references/worked-examples.md) only when metrics, industry transfer, or proposed scenarios need clarification.

## Build the answer bank

1. Read the actual JD and available company or interviewer material. Assign requirement IDs. Translate each requirement into the business outcome the employer appears to need; label interpretation as inference. A posting states a mandate, not evidence of an implemented system or a guaranteed fit.
2. Match each outcome to an actual example. Preserve the real employer, role, dates or duration, individual contribution, mechanism, result, units, and source limits. Prefer the closest proof to the question. Do not default every answer to the same employer or combine separate employers into one event.
3. Tag every claim and every requirement using the evidence contract. Where evidence permits, provide a primary and a backup story. If no real example is available, show the gap and a focused fact question; continue the supported work.
4. Write a short, ready-to-say answer. For historical questions: supported result → brief context → personal action and strategy → relevance to this role. Distinguish what Mike did from what the team delivered. Use Lucy's pause and outcome-first/internal-pivot methods with `SRC: c_o9EVzBiVE`; do not force a stock opener into every answer.
5. For proposed industry application, start with the intended business outcome and use first-person conditional/future wording throughout: “I would…” or “I'd…”. Name the decision, stakeholders, operating mechanism, and test for success. Keep it separate from the historical proof. A plausible retail scenario never becomes retail employment or an achieved retail result.
6. Review the whole JD for coverage and gaps. An answer can cover a discussion topic while leaving the underlying experience requirement unmet. State the strongest supported fit and the material gaps; do not promise a perfect fit, offer, or hiring outcome.

Use the requested length. Otherwise aim for roughly 40–65 words per primary answer, with optional detail held as follow-up notes. This length is Mike's preparation convention, not a Lucy quotation or universal rule. Preserve an essential qualification even if the answer needs a few more words. Stop after the point is clear.

## Deliver

- A requirement matrix: ID, actual requirement, inferred business outcome, primary/backup evidence IDs, fit status, answer ID, and open question.
- A speakable answer bank: question, answer, historical/proposed boundary, source IDs, and preparation guard. Keep preparation metadata outside the words Mike would say.
- A short gap list that separates missing evidence from established lack of experience. “Not established in these sources” is not proof something never happened.

Before handing it over, trace every material factual clause to an evidence ID. Check employer ownership, numeric meaning, time period, causal mechanism, and past/future tense. Remove unsupported clauses or label them as proposals. Phrase approval does not confirm an event occurred.

For resume drafting, use [lucy-resume-evidence](../lucy-resume-evidence/SKILL.md): pass supported history as evidence and keep new ideal-achievement patterns labeled until Mike maps his real examples. For other Lucy workflows, use the existing sibling `../lucy-skills/SKILL.md`; this leaf does not replace that package.
