# Creator playbook: positioning (lv-strategy)

What 11 creator doctrines (10 channels added 2026-09-28 + Tommy Clark) teach for this stage.
Synthesis in RIG's words; bracketed IDs are the source videos; every number below is a creator
claim and none of it is a receipt Mike can publish. All ten creators carry a positioning section;
none is skipped here.

## Consensus (3+ creators agree)

1. Narrow the account to one audience and one sentence a stranger can repeat. Clark opens with the
   golden question and scattered topics as the stall diagnosis [w2_zVhExo1s, tay_EcbOi7E]; Lara
   teaches one problem, one audience, one offer and wins the niche before widening [EACWADXrg88,
   qUmkSnXzW5A]; Pierre's triple is one audience, one expertise, one offer with a 20-second clarity
   test [wbVwUpopQgI, mV1Ma2ZVgU8]; Michelle wants a sentence so repeatable that someone else can
   say it for you [mDsy5N6jgHs]; Kasey restarts any sentence that describes a job title
   [aPa6qCvPFao, rGypgxFz3TM]; Harry wants one niche and one role a human and an AI reader can
   classify [sUC5UAXni6M, hWhYdDbcVCg]; Shanee narrows to one category and one ICP [bCRYL6aL3ec].
2. Write to one named buyer, never a crowd. Clark pictures one buyer and picks the reaction the post
   should earn [szN0DF3Oqik, XCT5Mubw4AI]; Kasey writes for 50 named decision makers or the person
   he was two levels ago [M0dAWh95NaQ, bCxD79Wl6ec]; Shanee derives the buyer from the top 20
   percent of payers and writes to their trigger moment [6f3ZzY5-gUg, bm6_uMvLagQ]; Mark holds
   1,000 fit names above 10,000 random ones [ArYphaJtvmc, 8BidcQ5viLs]; Pierre argues niche B2B
   needs no broad audience at all [zVC1HEH9060].
3. A topic earns its place only where buyer interest, offer proximity and credibility overlap.
   Clark's three-circle test [E5vxr1IZCpI, mTXBTznvNZI, vcFh5-fynHc, 1je0kCMMstg]; Lara adds
   content-offer fit so every post solves a problem the product solves [1ph8i-NDz4k, b_sVHSaQqGA];
   Kasey wants receipts and named transformations instead of opinions [rGypgxFz3TM]; Pierre refuses
   any post matching none of his pillars [vzQrN8BVE9c]; Shanee sells the first problem in the
   buyer's own words [DdJYKsAfWqE].
4. Choose the game before writing anything. Clark names leads, recruiting, fundraising or awareness
   and weights pillars to match [HFCfw6gznRM, vcFh5-fynHc]; Shanee starts from a 90-day business
   goal, then the buyer, then the assets [6f3ZzY5-gUg, DitOkxbdSvo]; Will lets the funnel stage
   decide each post [0_8TCrdBPVM, UJvAtV63vk0]; Kasey's line is that being known is not being paid
   [rGypgxFz3TM]; Diandra treats impressions as a means and pipeline as the score [w3_109ygioY,
   ZAqIUNAvXXo].
5. Repetition is what makes an account known for one thing. Clark repeats 1-3 core ideas for years
   and names his frameworks so prospects repeat the coined terms [2Yv8osegsKY, vcIAAUP6b8Y,
   TH63A9m1VNA]; Mark runs one repeated enemy the market already resents [FEOAeCCK9e0,
   T7G4_owju2A]; Pierre pairs a point of view with a named enemy [vzQrN8BVE9c]; Shanee keeps one
   repeated CTA and most posts on the one category [bCRYL6aL3ec]; Kasey wants 8 of 10 posts to tell
   a stranger what the account is about [iVnkmeVj9ao].
6. Broad, beginner or "for everyone" content pulls non-buyers. Clark's retold failure is an audience
   that would never buy [RpOt0kEWAFc, HFCfw6gznRM]; Pierre says broad funnel-stage content attracts
   followers who disengage the moment you sell [zVC1HEH9060, 2HSavr17yq0]; Shanee says beginner
   content attracts beginners [0cQdGNRvatM]; Kasey bans "for everyone" after measuring revenue per
   post [kuipymgFpjk]; Michelle cites research that a too-broad feed sells less [rW1CGbmLvzA].

   hWhYdDbcVCg]; 25 Sept 2026 praises a creator who refused a boxed buyer and grew faster
   [kK5H2Uu1L6Y]. The 25 Sept side is newer, but the doctrine file itself says not to copy the

## What nobody teaches

- No creator in the set teaches how to position one profile for three competing goals (influencer,
  job, clients) at once. Clark's choose-the-game assumes one game; this stage surfaces the
  trade-off to Mike instead of blending.
- Nobody gives a numeric kill criterion for retiring a positioning or a pillar. Their dated shifts
  are self-revisions, not thresholds; the numeric kill criteria stay in `references/pillars-and-lanes.md`.
- Nobody teaches positioning under a claim-audit constraint where each number needs a measured
  source. Their own figures are creator claims throughout, which is exactly why they cannot be
  copied into Mike's copy.
- Nobody covers a governed multi-lane estate where three lanes share one profile and never borrow
  each other's voice.
