## Length bands and week shape

- Length bands (2026): short under 500 characters, medium 500-1,500, long 1,500-3,000 (the platform
  limit). Rotate them. [QKNc515DSro]
- Three better posts a week is the current bar; the per-account ceiling is about one or two a day, and
  more volume means more accounts, not more posts. [s6KMjhznXqs, N2b0QVoCEPg]
- Funnel split about 20/60/20 (top/middle/bottom); at three posts that is one per stage. The 2026
  barbell alternative: one moonshot, one scrappy fast post, one experiment. [s6KMjhznXqs]
- Plan a week ahead; month-deep queues go stale. Batch-write on one day. [8nBIAUSIRnQ, creator: McTighe]
- A series is scheduled whole: every part committed before part one ships. [hook-families]

## CTA design (intent stage, friction, one ask)

1. Classify the current ask's mismatch: wrong intent stage, vague verb, hidden benefit, or too much
   friction. [content-cta-optimizer]
2. Count friction units in the ask: form fields, steps, calendar commitment, money.
3. Rewrite to one verb, one concrete outcome, one friction reducer, matched to awareness,
   consideration or decision stage.
4. Generate three variants, each with the hypothesis it tests (urgency, curiosity, risk reversal),
   and name the metric and window that separate them.
5. House rules that bound the rewrite: give four, take one; one primary CTA only; no ask in most
   posts; the CTA must map to the workflow goal (a job, followers, or clients); never ask the reader
   to do work for RIG before value is delivered; a PS-line link only when the post may be boosted.
   [content-cta-optimizer, /rig-ae-linkedin-run, hook-validation-prompts, viral-core]
6. Forbidden CTAs: "what do you think", "drop a comment", "follow me for more", and any engagement
   bait of the comment-word-and-I-will-send-it family. [/rig-ae-linkedin-run, hook-families]

## Threads and series

When one idea cannot hold inside a single post, structure it as a series rather than stretching it:
- Extract the spine: one claim, three to seven supporting beats, the strongest proof. [content-thread-structurer]
- Post one is a self-contained payoff statement, with no "thread below" throat-clearing.
- One payload per post; cut any post that only transitions. Each post must survive being screenshotted
  alone.
- Order the middle by escalating stakes; place the strongest proof at 60-70 percent through.
- Close with a one-line recap per beat plus the CTA, and verify the isolation check per post.
- On LinkedIn, a series is the cadence family: same chassis, escalating stakes, "Post N of M", every
  part scheduled before the first ships. [hook-families]
