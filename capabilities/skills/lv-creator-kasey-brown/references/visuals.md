# Visuals (Kasey Brown)

The image is the second hook, and he treats photo choice as a writing decision rather than decoration. Numbers below are creator claims.

## Photo as a stop device

- The photo and the first two lines are optimized together; he puts a large share of prep time into that pair, and calls the photo half of the first impression. [4jnS31Fh5QE, ycA0D55AEQ8]
- Raw beats polished in his testing: plain selfies and candid shots outperformed stock images and studio headshots by a wide margin (his example ranges: stock posts in the tens of likes, candid posts in the thousands). [yrI3QPIUk0Q]
- Uncropped screenshots with visible whitespace beat tight crops; old or unexpected photos grab attention; a portrait orientation takes more feed real estate and stops the thumb longer. [m9VFFCW_qZs, hC7ZCVsG7qM, yrI3QPIUk0Q]
- Contradiction works visually the same way it works in a line: an image that does not match the expected context (his audit example is an appliance in a classroom) creates the stop before anyone reads. [vBxw5q522pY]
- His newsjacking posts pair a time-contrast hook with a photo of the subject mid-action; the photo raises the open question that the text withholds. [LvA5pocucUM]

## What he does not teach

- Carousels are close to absent from his own teaching. The only carousel signal in the corpus is a guest (Chase Diamond) naming carousels and image-with-text posts as his best-performing formats in his own data. Treat that as a guest claim, not Kasey's doctrine. [DXqN9z7HK-w]
- He teaches no LinkedIn video specs, no design tooling and no brand-template system.

## Video

- Dated shift on native video: in Aug 2025 he says video is being pushed and tells people to test it, making vertical video on TikTok first and reposting it. [LvA5pocucUM] By Oct 2025 his guest reports video going from pushed to hit-or-miss and cuts it unless a sponsor requires it. [DXqN9z7HK-w]
- His video craft notes: every line in a video should re-hook, one case held attention by withholding the answer for about 35 seconds, and a text overlay hook carries the stop. [vBxw5q522pY]
- Short-form video as proof: one client's roughly 90-second video of a real hiring moment drove a large application spike and follower growth on both her profile and her company page. [lX3xNg2Ekw4]
- Long-form video lives off-platform for him: YouTube is the trust-depth channel and podcast or live appearances nurture what LinkedIn reaches. [iDK3ptwXCpo, _YfpJVRvWPI]
- Live or raw video is itself trust content: an unedited walk-and-talk and a filmed writing session serve as proof of authorship. [P_78dP1UWcI, jcxbBuiW2vE]

## Profile surfaces

- The cover image should state plainly what you do. [eyfNP4ipgrI]
- The banner is a targeting surface: a guest uses audience-specific objects in the banner so the right tribe recognizes itself before reading (his term is tribal density). [XNmTFbRmy_o]
- A repackaged lead magnet with a fresh hook and fresh visuals outperforms the original asset. [ycA0D55AEQ8]

## Applying it (RIG)

Choose the photo at the same moment as the hook, and prefer a real, unpolished image from Mike's own work over any generated graphic; if a visual must be generated, Eden is the assigned generator and it gets a real scene or receipt as input. Skip carousels in this doctrine and use image-with-text or a plain candid photo. Route the finished artifact through `rig-s1gate check post.linkedin`; the image judgment itself can go through `advisor-plus judge-image` when a vision check is worth it. Any client photo or screenshot needs the client's approval first, and nothing about a minor is ever in scope here.
