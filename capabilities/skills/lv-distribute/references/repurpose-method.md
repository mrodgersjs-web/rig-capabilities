# Repurpose method: one asset, many surfaces

## The claims ledger first

Before any platform version is written, extract the claims ledger: every claim that must survive the trip, with its evidence and its evidence class (`[primary] [secondary] [creator] [claimed] [ours]`). A version that drops a load-bearing claim or inflates one beyond its source is a defect, not a rewrite.

## Re-architect, do not re-intro

Each platform version gets its own opener, its own ordering and its own depth. Copy-pasting with a different first line is visibly recycled content, and the duplicate-content filter downstream makes it a delivery failure as well. Same claims, different construction. Then:

1. Select the native format per platform and its constraints (length, structure, hook style).
2. Score each version against the voice fingerprint; revise anything that drifts.
3. Verify the claims ledger against the finished versions: every claim present, none exaggerated.

## Fan-out ratios (target, not a promise)

The working shape for one strong source asset is roughly one long piece into ten short posts, five articles and three threads. Treat those as planning ratios for a repurposing pass, not as a measured outcome, and quality-score each derivative instead of hitting the count.

## Podcasts (the relationship channel)

Podcast distribution is a pitch, not a repost. What earns a booking: a specific contrarian angle rather than an offer of expertise, one data point that makes the host look smart for booking the guest, and evidence the guest can hold a conversation (a video, a webinar, a prior interview). Target shows that interview practitioners rather than only authors, with more than 50 episodes and an episode published in the last 30 days. Keep the target list with host, contact, episode count, relevance score and outreach status in the lane's project page.

## What this method refuses

- A version whose claims do not survive verification.
- A version that reads like the LinkedIn post with a new first line.
- Any derivative that carries a lane's voice into another lane's audience.
- Reporting the fan-out count as a result; only a moved metric is an output.
