# Writing (Pierre Herubel)

## The writing process

Five steps in a fixed order, plus an image step: find a fresh insight, give it one angle and one idea, choose a skimmable structure, write the hook, then deliver exactly what the hook promised in simple sentences and short words, closing on a question or a reaction prompt [i-YDUfx84f8]. One post equals one idea; he compares the process to a journalist angling a sourced fact [i-YDUfx84f8, BkdOFSfFPD4].

Insight sources he repeats: client and prospect questions, your own experiments and build-in-public work, your backstory, competitor gaps, events, communities and customer research [i-YDUfx84f8, BkdOFSfFPD4, 9o4BqmpgVT8]. He also logs one surprising line or question after every sales call (he mentions running an AI pass over his calls so nothing is missed) and claims a typical call holds 5 to 10 usable insights (creator claim) [9o4BqmpgVT8].

## Structures and post types

- Copy frameworks taught: problem-agitate-solution, before-after-bridge, AIDA, and a storytelling frame of situation, problem, story. AIDA and the story frame were added in March 2025; earlier videos named only the first two [SmuGlt1CDKY, i-YDUfx84f8].
- Five expertise post formats for B2B: a named framework explained, an educated opinion built on observed patterns, a why-you-should case assembled as a carousel covering outcomes and the cost of inaction, problem-agitate-solution as a standalone post, and a behind-the-screen backstory tied to your expertise [jqqofSszVfI].
- Expert storytelling in seven shapes: backstory summary, opinion on a trend inside your expertise, a zoom-out point of view gathered over years, a how-I piece running problem to solution to messy implementation to end state, company or career vision, build-in-public, and a lessons list where every item carries the anecdote that taught it [YIOMfYkRE-U]. Dated shift: in July 2025 he moved personal story from a small post-authority slice to a growth engine he credits with his takeoff, while still refusing random life updates [YIOMfYkRE-U].
- Ranked format ladder from his own 14-day test (353,000 impressions across 10 posts, creator claim): poll, topic infographic, photo with a rules list, checklist infographic, comparison table, carousel list, carousel walkthrough, macro strategy map, headline-driven infographic, and a rerun of a proven hero infographic [CphfSIw7WV8].
- The 444 matrix (July 2026): every post is one format (screenshot, infographic, carousel, photo with text) times one idea source (client conversations, industry trends, your experiments, your frameworks) times one angle (opinion, how-to, list, story or case study), which he counts as 64 repeatable recipes (creator claim). Test 3 to 5 combinations and keep the winners [9o4BqmpgVT8].

## Length, readability, voice

- He writes long posts on purpose; length is fine when the pain or objective is one the reader already cares about [Io7Uq-S8dpk].
- Readers skim and only re-read when the structure earns it. Rules kept to a maximum of two lines, short sentences, line breaks, bullets, and a blank line between items for mobile readability [SmuGlt1CDKY, BkdOFSfFPD4, mV1Ma2ZVgU8].
- Payload goes into short paragraphs and bullets; bold the key takeaway with a short tagline; never let a complex post carry low value [BkdOFSfFPD4, 2eovlLOHOU4].
- Simple words, no filler, and no AI-generic content: he claims content must be about 10 times more interesting and unique than the feed average or it reads as machine noise (creator claim on the multiplier) [mV1Ma2ZVgU8, c_2g0KM7fqY].
- Attention credits: readers spend a finite attention budget per session (he illustrates 10 credits per 10 minutes, creator claim), so every post is scored on three questions: does the hook attract, does the body retain, was it worth the attention [c_2g0KM7fqY].

## CTAs

- Every post closes on one explicit next step, and the last carousel slide carries it too [3sf8fojVfpM, i-YDUfx84f8].
- Three CTA grades he names: soft (like or follow), medium (comment a keyword or send a DM), hard (book a call) [3sf8fojVfpM].
- Offer posts carry exactly one named objective and one CTA, and the offer must be a granular client problem, never a commodity in a crowded market [zVC1HEH9060, 2HSavr17yq0, h9BQP6-IzZo].
- The follow CTA names the benefit and the cadence [DB-P9AUogXU].
- Never disguise a pitch as content; pair every concept with a pragmatic example and bridge to the commercial action honestly [6RJb6qYq1gA].
