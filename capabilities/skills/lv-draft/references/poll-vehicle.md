# Polls as a vehicle: construction, cadence and the lead read-out

Distilled 2026-10-01 from three how-to transcripts. Every law carries
`CLAIM(<videoId>@mm:ss)`. Status is `consensus` where two or more videos agree and
`single-source` where one does. **Almost every claim below is asserted by the speaker with no data
shown**; that is recorded per law and it is the honest strength of this evidence. This module
supplies construction mechanics. It does not override the account's own measured results.

## Why this module exists

On 2026-09-17 one LinkedIn post passed 10K views (6.4K reach, 96 likes, 58 comments, 5 shares).
Every non-poll post between 2026-09-25 and 09-30 drew 44 to 116 impressions. The thesis was the
same in both. The vehicle was not. The 10K post closed on a four-option poll, and 58 of its
comments came from that one-tap vote. Before this module, no rule in this family covered polls at
all. [measured:eden-analytics-2026-10-01]

## Question

- **One line, plain language, answerable instantly by your industry and by a wider audience.**
  `CLAIM(CJr8lpbcllM@02:50)` "recommend that you keep your question short ideally just one line long".
  `CLAIM(Mowwpb3u39s@01:36)` "having a yes no style answer or a very simple to answer".
  `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@03:30)` "stay away from technical jargon or anything else that might confuse
  people". **consensus (3 videos). Asserted, no data shown.**
- **Hard ceiling: 140 characters on the question.** `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@03:30)`
  "there's 140-character limit for questions". **single-source.**
- **If answering takes thought, the poll dies in the feed.** `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@03:38)` "if people
  have to work to figure out what you're asking they're just going to skip that poll". **single-source,
  asserted, no data shown.** Mechanistically consistent with the line above.
- **Never ask a personal question. Generalise it.** `CLAIM(Mowwpb3u39s@01:45)` "making sure you're
  not asking something personal about it". His own comparison: "do you sleep badly?" drew fewer
  votes than "how many hours should people sleep?" `CLAIM(Mowwpb3u39s@02:35)`. **single-source; one
  screen-share of his own poll (77 votes), no controlled comparison. Asserted, no data shown.**
- **Do not ask leading questions; you want the answers honest.** `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180)` research-goal
  section: "don't ask leading questions that suggest that people should answer a certain way".
  **single-source.**

## Options

- **Three options, or the maximum four.** `CLAIM(CJr8lpbcllM@03:19)` "provide at least three if not
  all four". `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@04:02)` "your poll will also stand out in the newsfeed if it takes up
  more vertical space... I recommend using three, if not the maximum four". **consensus (2). Both
  assert the feed-space mechanism; neither shows data.** Hard ceiling is four
  (`CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@04:02)`), and answers are capped at **30 characters** each.
- **Make the fourth option "Other / Let me know" with a downward arrow.** `CLAIM(CJr8lpbcllM@05:22)`
  "let me know CU I want to encourage people to actually leave a comment".
  `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@04:16)` "the fourth option says 'Other' or 'Let me know' with an arrow or a hand
  pointing down". **consensus (2), asserted, no data shown.**
- **Emojis in the answer options, where they fit the brand.** `CLAIM(CJr8lpbcllM@03:34)`
  `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@03:52)` "try to squeeze emojis into the answers if you can, if it fits with your
  brand". **consensus (2), with the brand-fit caveat from hzKlBu_y180.**
- **Questions and answers cannot be edited after posting.** `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180)` setup walkthrough:
  "you will not be able to edit your questions or answers later. So now's the time to proofread like
  crazy." **single-source.** This is a real failure mode and belongs in the pre-flight check.

## Caption above the poll

- **Explain why you are asking. Some people vote just to help you.** `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@02:50)`
  "explain why you're doing the poll". He suggests leading statements like "I'm curious" or "I'm
  doing research". **single-source.**
- **Key line in the first two to three lines, above the "see more" fold.**
  `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@03:03)` "make sure this key information is in the first two to three lines of
  your caption so it doesn't get cut off by '...see more.'" **single-source.** Consistent with this
  family's existing fold rule.
- **Ask for the vote outright.** `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@03:10)` "include a call-to-action that flat out
  asks people to vote". **single-source.**
- **Keep the caption short so the poll is not buried under it.** `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@03:17)` "keep
  your caption short and sweet". **single-source.**

## Cadence: the corpus genuinely disagrees, and the dissent is calibrated to our exact cadence

- **For weekly:** `CLAIM(Mowwpb3u39s@00:24)` "We do a poll every single Monday. The reason why is
  that we want to spike the algorithm" and `CLAIM(CJr8lpbcllM@06:04)` "start using polls in your
  content every single week". **consensus (2) on weekly; the spike-the-algorithm mechanism is
  asserted, no data shown.**
- **Against weekly:** `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@04:48)` "keep your polls just a small part of your total
  content mix. **Let's say in general, you post three times a week. Maybe you want to make a poll
  about once a month.** If your audience sees polls too often from you, those polls aren't going to
  feel special anymore and your audience is just going to get tired of them." **single-source, but
  this is the only claim in the corpus that is explicitly calibrated to a three-posts-a-week
  account, which is this account's actual cadence.**
- **This is a live, unadjudicated conflict. Do not resolve it by picking the louder claim.** At
  three posts a week, a weekly poll is a third of all output. Recommendation for this account until
  measured: **one poll per month, treated as the month's one high-engagement vehicle**, per
  `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@04:48)`. That is a recommendation, not a measured result, and it is testable:
  run two polls a month apart and compare against the 44-116 impression baseline.

## Duration

- **Choices are 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks. Never 1 day.** `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@08:32)` "one day,
  in my opinion, is way too short. Three days is pretty good because you're going to get most of
  your answers in the first three days." He calls 1 week good for more responses and 2 weeks if
  there is no rush. **single-source.**
- **One week is the default for volume.** `CLAIM(CJr8lpbcllM@05:34)` "I always leave it to be out
  for a week". **Mild conflict:** 3 days is the urgency option, 1 week is the volume option. Both
  agree the floor is above 1 day.

## During and after the poll

- **Seed a first comment and reply to every comment.** `CLAIM(Mowwpb3u39s@05:08)` "it's nice to get
  a first comment in to get the conversation going". `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@04:27)` "always, always,
  always respond to comments in the poll". **consensus (2), asserted, no data shown.**
- **Publish a results-and-insights post once the poll closes.** `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180)` closing
  walkthrough: "once you've collected all your results... do a separate LinkedIn post that talks
  about the results and shares your insights about them. It's the type of conversation that makes
  the LinkedIn algorithm happy." **single-source, asserted, no data shown** but it costs nothing
  and reuses data already collected.
- **Copy the post link and share it off-platform while the poll runs.** `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180)` "once
  the poll is published, you can click on the three dots and then Copy link to post". **single-source.**

## The lead read-out (the part most people miss)

- **Only the poll creator sees who voted for what** (or company-page admins if posted from a page).
  `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@06:27)` "after a poll is published, you'll be able to see who exactly voted for
  what... only the person who created the poll... or the admins of that company page."
  `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@06:42)` "this information is gold when it comes to qualifying leads".
  **single-source.** Treat the breakdown as a private warm-lead list.
- **You do not need many votes.** `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@07:33)` "you don't need thousands of votes. You
  just need enough leads to have quality conversations." **single-source.**
- **Follow up conversation-first. Never open with a pitch.** `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@06:59)` "the worst
  thing you should do is immediately launch into a sales pitch". Give something that answers the
  problem their vote revealed: "a tip sheet, or an ebook, or a white paper, or a video link"
  `CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@07:10)`. Reference their specific vote `CLAIM(Mowwpb3u39s@05:37)`, and vary the
  copy so it is not identical to everyone else's `CLAIM(Mowwpb3u39s@06:10)`. **consensus (2),
  asserted, no data shown.**
- **Governing rules that this section inherits and cannot weaken:** no DMs or off-platform sends
  from a client lane without Mike's per-batch yes, with the exact text shown first. LinkedIn
  connection-request and messaging automation is covered by the platform's own ban on automated
  engagement. This module describes what a human may do by hand; it does not authorise automation.

## Pick one goal per poll

`CLAIM(hzKlBu_y180@00:44)` "pick one of these benefits as your most important goal so you can have
laser focus" for engagement, market research, authority, or leads. "Aimless polls rarely hit any
target." **single-source.**

## What this module explicitly does not claim

No speaker in this corpus shows a controlled comparison, a baseline, or a dataset. The strongest
number offered anywhere is `CLAIM(CJr8lpbcllM@01:06)` "over 28,000 views in only 24 hours" for one
poll, **asserted, no data shown**. Nothing here should be reported as a measured result on our
accounts. The one measured fact this module rests on is ours: the only post to clear 10K in three
weeks used this vehicle, and nothing since has tried it.
