# Image system: photos, graphics, GIFs, quote cards, banner

## THE HOOK GOES ON THE IMAGE (Mike, 2026-10-05, after the Hotel California post)

**Rule: when a post has a hook or a catchphrase, the image carries that hook or catchphrase. An image that
shares no signifiers with the post text is a second, unrelated post and it fails the brief.**

This supersedes any standing "no text overlay" instruction for the hook line only. Text on the image is
now required for a hooked post, and forbidden for everything else on the canvas.

What went wrong on Mon 2026-10-05, so it is not repeated:
- The post opened on the Eagles lyric "we are programmed to receive... you can never leave" and the
  conceit of a place you can check out of but never leave.
- The generated image was a Pixar French bulldog asleep on a stack of dog food bags at sunset. It was a
  good picture of the post's *last paragraph*. It carried none of the hook: no hotel, no night man, no
  "relax," no departure sign, no highway, no Beverly Hills.
- A reader who saw only the image, and a reader who saw only the text, got two different posts.

### The three-part image brief every hooked post now carries

1. **Hook line.** The exact words of the hook or catchphrase, 3 to 9 words, on the image. Never
   paraphrased. If the hook is a lyric or a quoted line, use the quoted words and credit the source in
   the post text.
2. **Hook signifiers.** At least two visual objects that only that hook would produce. For Hotel
   California: hotel corridor at night, a lit EXIT sign seen from inside, a valet stand, an
   open-air lobby palm, the amber dusk on a coastal highway, a key rack, luggage nobody collects.
   For The Karate Kid: the balance-post on the water, a gi sleeve, wax and a rag. If you cannot name two,
   the hook is too vague to illustrate and the post needs a stronger hook first.
3. **Cast reference.** Any recurring character is passed as an input image, never described in words
   alone. Birch is the Pixar French bulldog in the Higgsfield asset library, asset
   `6378fca3-ff3a-4c5a-8233-f14403e88c7c` in the "RIG" folder. Text-only "a merle French bulldog in a
   red cape" produces a different dog every time and is the failure this replaces.

### The rest of the canvas stays clean

One message per image. The hook line is the message. No second slogan, no hashtag block, no watermark,
no handles, no fake UI, no AI-infographic chrome. Type is a single line, set like a film poster title
card or a sign in the scene, not a caption bar. Legible at feed size on a phone: if the hook line does
not read cleanly at roughly 400px wide, shorten it rather than shrinking the type.

### Honest status of this rule

This is a correction from a failed post, not a measured win. There is no data yet showing a hook line on
a LinkedIn image beats a clean image. It is in force because Mike decided it, and it carries the same
day-30 kill rule as the rest of the lane: at day 30, compare median reach of hooked-image posts against
clean-image posts. If hooked images do not win, the text comes off and this section is deleted rather
than softened.

# Image system: photos, graphics, GIFs, quote cards, banner

## The default image is a real photo of Mike

The post-engine corpus is unanimous: the default LinkedIn post image is a real photo of Mike, not stock
and not an AI infographic. A graphic earns its place only when the payload is a framework, a stat or a
quick insight [image-system]. Clark's evidence agrees: IRL photos of real people and places out-pull
branded graphics, which read as ads [szN0DF3Oqik].

Photo production notes: a composite of a cutout over a brightened layer on a blurred background reads
cleaner than a raw snapshot; the profile photo rides on every comment, so treat a colored backdrop as
the brand logo; face about 60% of frame, head and shoulders, window light. Any platform statistic a
creator quotes about photo performance stays a creator claim and never enters artwork [image-system].

## What stops the scroll

A face is the strongest static scroll-stopper, which is also why covers carry a face. Big, bold, short
title text; one message per image; at most 8 words of text on a single graphic; one consistent palette
across banner, slides, graphics and GIF thumbnails so the brand reads at thumbnail size [image-system].

## GIFs

Use for a quick loop of one stat or insight, a before/after, or a micro-demonstration. Same rules as
graphics: one message, burned caption if text matters, at most a 6-second loop, palette locked
[image-system].

## Graphics: pick the construction path

Decision: numbered steps, frameworks, comparisons or data tables go to a structured HTML/CSS graphic;
a recap, workflow, tip set or story goes to a generated infographic [graphic-designer].

**Structured HTML/CSS graphic:** fixed portrait canvas around 1200x1400, dark or brand background with
high-contrast text, one accent color, at least 40px padding, no stock-photo backgrounds. The post
dictates the block count; the constraint is legibility on a phone. Single self-contained HTML file with
inline CSS; distil the post into a short headline (5-8 words) plus visual blocks, never a full copy.
Open it in a browser and screenshot for export [graphic-designer].

**Generated infographic:** the graphic recaps the post, it is not an abstract illustration. Extract the
shortened headline, 3-6 key points and any highlight numbers, then write a self-contained prompt for
either a hand-drawn whiteboard look (photographed marker texture, imperfect lines, multi-color pens) or
a flat branded look (clean editorial, no 3D, no gradients). Cap the entire generated image at about 40
words of text; no decorative borders, no watermarks [graphic-designer].

## Quote cards

Quote posts pair a short line with a recreated visual style. Constraints: the line stays under 15
words, punches in its first three words, works standalone; the card renders at 1080x1350 with the quote
as the focal point and no names, handles or logos unless sourced. Quotes are written fresh; never
fabricate attribution to a real person. Expect strong engagement but lower impressions than other
formats and say so honestly [quote-post]. If the house voice is explicitly not motivational, flag the
mismatch before generating anything [quote-post].

## Brand-asset pattern work

When borrowing a viral graphic idea: replicate the pattern, never the asset. Copying a competitor's
graphic and re-skinning it is plagiarism and reads generic anyway. A brand kit of 1-3 example images
defines the style; output stays editable where possible [image-system].

## Multi-image posts

The composer supports selecting several images at once; use sparingly because multiple frames dilute
the single-message rule. Prefer a carousel past two frames of content [image-system].

## Banner (profile, one-time plus refresh)

The banner is the profile's digital billboard. It carries a clear one-line message, industry-relevant
visuals, one palette matching the profile, and one CTA route. Mistakes: pixelation, crowded text, poor
contrast. Use the generator's LinkedIn-banner preset and verify the render; creator-cited dimensions
are unreliable, so do not hand-pixel from them. The job-seeker variant is a simple template with name
plus two or three descriptors (title, top skills, passion, one personal interest) [image-system].

Profile funnel note: video and carousels drive profile visits and conversion happens on the profile,
so banner and featured work is high-leverage [image-system, video-shorts].

## Stock sourcing and link previews

Original images are best; stock is the fallback. When using stock, take images with a no-known-
restrictions licence and re-check the licence before publishing anyway. Free stock sites interleave
paid referral results, so sort carefully. Keep several bookmarked sources so one miss is not a blocker
[image-system]. A clickable link preview auto-imports text from the destination page: read the preview
text before shipping, and if it is bad, drop the preview and put a plain text link at the end of the
caption instead [image-system].

## Palette checklist

- One palette across banner, carousels, graphics and GIF thumbnails
- Typography matches the house fonts
- An exported identity page (palette, type, tone) rides with every Eden brief so the generator inherits
  the system instead of ad-hoc descriptions
- Lane A and Lane B assets never share cast or mascot elements
- No watermarks, no template-stock artifacts, no QA text
