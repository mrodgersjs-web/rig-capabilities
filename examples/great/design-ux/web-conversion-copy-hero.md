# Great example - web-conversion-copy-hero

The hero block for the Northwind Billing page, above the fold at 1440x900 and 390x844.

What makes it excellent:

1. One promise, one proof, one action, and nothing else above the fold.

   H1 (the promise, 9 words): "Close the month in one day, not four."
   Subhead (the mechanism, 22 words): "Northwind Billing matches every invoice against
   your ledger export nightly and hands your controller only the exceptions, so the close
   stops being a four-day fire drill."
   Primary CTA (the action): "Book a reconciliation review", with the reassurance line
   "Twenty minutes. No card. We read one month of your ledger live."
   Secondary CTA: none above the fold. A ghost action "See pricing" sits below the first
   screen.

2. It passes the five-second test at 3/3. Shown the hero for five seconds and then hidden,
   a finance controller answered what they get (close in a day), who it is for (controllers
   at mid-size companies), and what to click (the book button) without prompting.

3. It passes the squint test. Blurred to 8px, the H1 and the primary button are the only
   two things that still read. The wordmark is 30% of the H1 cap height and sits top left
   where it cannot compete.

4. The hierarchy is numeric and deliberate. Body 18px, H1 58px (3.2x), subhead 24px
   (1.33x), CTA label 19px bold (1.05x). H1 line height 1.08, body 1.6. One accent colour,
   spent entirely on the primary button.

5. Both viewports keep the action visible. At 1440x900 the button sits at 71% of the
   viewport height. At 390x844 the hero image moves behind the copy at 22% opacity and the
   button sits at 76%, still without scrolling.

The result to beat: the three answers come back correct from a cold stranger, and no
control above the fold offers a second action.
