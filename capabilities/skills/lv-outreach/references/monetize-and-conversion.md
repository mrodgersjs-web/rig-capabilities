# Monetization and the client engine

The thing after the reply: what is being sold, what a cold click lands on, how a booked call is
prepared and kept, what the terms do, and the weekly loop that runs content and outreach as one
decision.

## Offer construction

The offer is centered on the prospect, not on us: `offer_prospect_centred` proxies second-person
against first-person plural and a pass means keep reading, not good. Honesty opens the door and
the offer of free work closes it, but free work is a pricing instrument, not generosity: know
which one you are doing. For RIG the fitting shape is a bounded paid-equivalent artefact (one
named diagnostic, real, time-boxed), not open spec work. Scarcity attaches by slot count. The DM
payload is the offer: an offer that cannot travel in a DM is an offer defect. Separate the job of
each asset: a post creates curiosity, a quiz qualifies and educates, a DM is not a proposal
[offer-construction].

## Quiz and assessment funnels

Magnets fail silently: PDFs unread past page two, webinars unattended, booked calls that never
show; a sign-up metric cannot see any of it, so measure completion and then conversation. A quiz
makes the visitor self-diagnose before anyone sells, and every answer is lead intelligence (pain,
budget, urgency). `cold_traffic_routes_to_quiz` refuses cold traffic pointed at a call, demo,
booking page or checkout; warm traffic may go straight to a conversation. Front existing assets
with a quiz rather than retiring them (the PDF becomes the post-quiz gift). Claimed completion
figures (10-15% landing page vs 80-90% quiz; single-case numbers around 91%, 83%, 2 pounds a
lead) are vendor-adjacent and never quotable. RIG decides the carrier: a quiz is a form plus a
scoring rule, and a third-party platform is duplication [quiz-funnel].

## Owned audience

Rent (the feed) vanishes when posting stops; borrow (comment sections where attention already
exists) is pacing-bound; own (a list you can address when the platform changes) is the only layer
that is both distribution and conversion surface. RIG's owned layer is the thinnest one it has;
before any capture mechanic, Mike decides the canonical list, the promise at capture, and the
cadence RIG can actually sustain. Capture comes after the quiz exists and before the campaign
scales. An email list that never receives anything is a PDF magnet in another format
[owned-audience].

## Pre-call briefing

A brief must carry: their own words verbatim with URL and date; the observed change with the date
it was seen; three genuine gaps as questions; the two-way risk (what they evaluate you on, what
you cannot yet prove); and the specific ask this call tries to earn. No invented facts about a
named person or company: anything unread is `unknown`; a surface that failed to render is unknown,
not empty; re-read live sources before the call if they are more than a few days old. This is the
same artefact as Mike's live-call counterparty brief: one format, two consumers [call-prep].

## Contract terms as revenue instruments (not legal advice; Gate-D for Mike)

Options to pressure-test: a 3-month notice period (revenue during notice, runway to replace,
win-back window); zero performance guarantees (lead definitions are arguable and meetings no-show);
invoice the month ahead on auto-debit; one function per owner with a number by their name (applies
to the agent fleet too). Fix retention before pouring in acquisition volume: churn is the binding
constraint on a retainer base. Ordering rule for spend: no paid volume before an offer is proven
organically; paid volume turns an unproven offer into an expensive misunderstanding
[contract-terms].

## The client engine weekly loop

One week of client-getting work, planned and staged, nothing published or sent from the loop
itself [lg-client-engine]:

1. PLAN (Monday): 3-5 posts, exactly ONE ask-bearing post; plan the warm-queue sources in the
   same block.
2. DRAFT + WRITE each post into staging (stage mechanics stay in the draft stage).
3. SOURCE the week's warm queue from four channels: own comment section, post engagers, profile
   viewers, and a curated 20-30 creator comment list. Every candidate through the ICP fit card;
   no card, no name.
4. FOLLOWUP (daily): a 30-minute comment block around publishing plus DM drafting; drafts only.
5. CLOSE (Friday): reply rates per batch and per post; change one variable set at a time so next
   week's delta is attributable.

Numbers the loop may plan on, all creator claims: 3-5 posts a week (quality-capped, not daily),
hook visible before the fold (about 140 characters), first ~50 words carry feed candidacy, 30-60
seconds of profile research per prospect before any DM, one question per DM, 3-message ceiling,
30-minute comment block, same-day replies to comments. Platform limits are dated observations:
re-probe before quoting [lg-client-engine].
