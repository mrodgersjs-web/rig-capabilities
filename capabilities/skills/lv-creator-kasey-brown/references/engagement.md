# Engagement (Kasey Brown)

Commenting, replying, and the bridge from a public thread to a private conversation. All volume numbers are creator claims and they drift across videos; the drift is dated below.

## Commenting is a primary surface

- He treats comments as revenue work, not a metric: his commenting playbook is credited with roughly $1.2M in LinkedIn-relationship revenue and with most of his agency's inbound. [vc3FaWfXggY]
- Quality over volume: 5 to 15 comments a day on chosen people (ideal clients, collaborators, creators you want a relationship with, target industries), explicitly mocking the mainstream 50-a-day volume advice as generic noise that trains people to scroll past you. [vc3FaWfXggY]
- The comment standard is the coffee-shop test: only write what you would say to the person across a table. Add your own insight or a sharp question instead of praise; insight over flattery positions you as a peer and opens a conversation instead of closing one. [vc3FaWfXggY, uHgHT0fg3As]
- Comments warm the account before you post: 15 minutes of commenting before publishing, and a first month that is engagement-only (his early plan was 10-20 comments a day and 20 new connections a day) so the first real post lands into relationships. [uHgHT0fg3As]
- Comments borrow the poster's audience and put you in the rooms you want; they are not for engagement metrics. [aPa6qCvPFao]
- A single comment can outperform posts: one case earned about 40,000 impressions from one comment, and a guest reports comment impressions in the tens of thousands routinely. [S8gabIoWUDQ, DXqN9z7HK-w]
- Comment volume drift with dates: 10-20 a day for a beginner warm-up (2025-10) [uHgHT0fg3As]; 5-15 a day as the paid playbook (2025-07) [vc3FaWfXggY]; 5 thoughtful a day on a studied 50-person list (2026-05) [aPa6qCvPFao]; 20 a day inside a 20/20/20 daily challenge (2025-12) [7vl7hhn_y78]; a guest's 20-50 a day at scale (2025-10) [DXqN9z7HK-w]. Same doctrine, different intensity by goal.
- No pods and no engagement trades; a guest who scaled to 400,000 followers explicitly rejects them. [DXqN9z7HK-w]

## Replies and hour one

- His named rule is to reply to every comment in the first 60 to 90 minutes after posting, both as decency and as distribution; he admits failing at it on his own biggest post because it moved too fast. [hC7ZCVsG7qM]
- Reply to everyone, and if the post lacks a question, seed one in the comments to keep the thread alive. [4jnS31Fh5QE, uHgHT0fg3As]
- Simple reply behavior is the move most creators skip, and it is visible in the accounts he audits. [m9VFFCW_qZs]

## From comment to conversation

- The conversion path he repeats: a thoughtful comment signals interest, the DM opens with something personal and useful, and money travels comment, DM, call, paid work. His highest-value clients all arrived that way. [2t-EBcS43Lw, B18w0jPdXiI]
- Comment-keyword delivery: readers comment a keyword, then a DM delivers the asset; the team helps manage the influx at publish time. [jcxbBuiW2vE, jYCnMnYF9EE]
- Replies and DMs after a raw post are the whole play for expert beginners: one case turned a personal post into long threads and inbound invites. [bTGyrxFkB3g, u3j9X_QaJ6o]

## Conflicts with RIG rules

- Mike's comment rule is positive-only on other people's posts. Kasey teaches respectful challenges and contrarian counterpoints in comments [uHgHT0fg3As, vc3FaWfXggY]; on Mike's accounts the challenge becomes a positive validation plus one added insight, and no comment is AI-written. His comment-keyword automation is a send and waits for Mike's yes.

## Applying it (RIG)

Build a target list first (5 to 50 named people depending on the goal), comment 5 to 15 times a day with an insight or a question, and set a reminder to reply to every commenter on Mike's own posts for the first 90 minutes. Comments on other people's posts pass `rig-s1gate check comment.linkedin` and stay positive. Anything that moves a thread into DMs is engagement work logged in `linkedin-analytics-tracker`, and automated DM delivery stays off until Mike approves it.
