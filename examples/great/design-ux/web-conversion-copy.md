# Great example - web-conversion-copy

The full copy deck for a fictional B2B page, "Northwind Billing" invoice reconciliation,
written before any layout was drawn.

What makes it excellent:

1. The page has one job and it is written down. "Get a finance controller at a 50 to 500
   seat company to book a twenty-minute reconciliation review, because month-end close
   currently takes their team four days." Every block on the page is checked against that
   sentence and two blocks were cut because they did not serve it.

2. The copy order matches the decision order in the buyer's head. What is this (H1), is it
   for me (subhead naming the finance controller and the four-day close), why believe it
   (two named results and one case study), what do I do (one primary action), what if it
   goes wrong (three objections answered inline and a risk reversal beside the button).

3. One primary action, and the vocabulary is the buyer's. The button reads "Book a
   reconciliation review", the exact phrase three of five sales calls used. It does not
   read "Get started" or "Learn more". A secondary outline action offers the pricing page
   and appears once, below the first screen.

4. Features, benefits and mechanism are all written, then placed deliberately. The feature
   ("line-level matching against the ledger") is shown in the screenshot, the benefit
   ("close in one day instead of four") carries the headline, and the mechanism ("it reads
   your ledger export nightly and flags only the exceptions") sits in the supporting line
   for the sceptic.

5. Every conversion claim in the deck is labelled as a recommendation, never as a measured
   result. The one number on the page, "four days to one day", is quoted from a named
   customer with their permission and is not presented as an average.

The result to beat: read the deck aloud to a finance controller and they repeat the offer
back in their own words, and the only action available to them is the one the page wants.
