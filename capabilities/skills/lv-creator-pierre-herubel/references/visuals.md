# Visuals (Pierre Herubel)

His core belief: one concept per image, executed well, does more for a post than the copy around it. Design was his first career (eight years, creator claim), and adding infographics to a text-only feed is what he credits with his takeoff [vzQrN8BVE9c, 0ZKNmQnMQ7k, CphfSIw7WV8].

## Infographics

- Anatomy he builds to (label mine): an identity block, a hook line, one big titled diagram, a two-line legend with the takeaway, and post copy that mirrors the legend [0ZKNmQnMQ7k].
- What makes one perform: a relatable concept (a symptom whose cause the reader cannot locate), a framing title carrying a number and an evocative noun that implies depth, genuinely well-executed design, and copy plus legend that hold attention after the scroll [0ZKNmQnMQ7k].
- Attribute the image to your face, your name and a value-proposition tagline so a screenshot stays credited [0ZKNmQnMQ7k].
- Reuse is part of the asset: he reports posting the same infographic four times on LinkedIn and also shipping it in a newsletter, an ebook and a course (creator claim on the four placements) [0ZKNmQnMQ7k]. He reports hundreds of infographics made in total, generating millions of views (creator claim) [0ZKNmQnMQ7k].
- The 1.9M-impression post (creator claim) was a text argument plus one two-panel infographic: the wrong broad goal on the left, the mapped process on the right, built in Canva [Io7Uq-S8dpk].
- Deliberate debatability: he simplifies the numbers inside a diagram so experts dispute the percentages in comments, which spawns threads. Expert posts invite that debate; personal story posts do not [Io7Uq-S8dpk].
- Other diagram shapes he names: comparison tables, quadrant graphs, workflows, checklists, a layered iceberg of four stacked problem levels, and a macro strategy map whose sections each become a standalone later post [0ZKNmQnMQ7k, CphfSIw7WV8, BkdOFSfFPD4].
- Checklist and comparison formats package process knowledge; a macro map stays at the strategic level precisely so it can be cut up [CphfSIw7WV8].

## Carousels

- A carousel is a multi-page PDF read like a short book [jqqofSszVfI]. He uses two shapes: a plain list, and a page-by-page walkthrough [CphfSIw7WV8].
- Length guidance is inconsistent and worth knowing: a typical 5 to 10 slides, while one caption line argues for at least 10 slides to stretch dwell time (both creator claims, July 2026) [9o4BqmpgVT8].
- He names carousels as the strongest follower-growth format but caps them at two per week so the feed does not tire (creator claim: 2023 carousels produced 10,000+ followers in about three months) [DB-P9AUogXU].
- The last slide carries the CTA [3sf8fojVfpM]. Framework and visual-storytelling posts are the natural carousel jobs [BkdOFSfFPD4, jqqofSszVfI].

## Photos and screenshots

- Real photographs of you beat stock and generic art; one two-hour shoot yields 50 to 100 usable pictures for 3 to 6 months (creator claim) [jqqofSszVfI]. A photo of you with a short overlaid tagline summarizing the system is his standard support image [2eovlLOHOU4, CphfSIw7WV8].
- Screenshots of real artifacts (a client thank-you message, an analytics result, a comment, a notification, a chat message, a news headline with your read, a public post you react to) beat polished designs because they look real; pair them with a short overlaid comment carrying your take [9o4BqmpgVT8]. Dated shift: screenshots enter his top format tier only in July 2026, after two years centered on infographics and carousels [9o4BqmpgVT8].
- Candid photos of you at work or at events cut through a stiff feed and let success and behind-the-scenes land [9o4BqmpgVT8].

## Video

- He teaches no LinkedIn-native video doctrine in this corpus. Video appears only as format-diversity filler and as one asset type: a roughly 10-minute video sales letter recorded in a quiet room and delivered from a landing page (creator claim on length) [5wmCL8tA04I, DB-P9AUogXU, 2QbMkODGVPw].
- For teams, his central content factory produces infographics, carousels, thought-leadership posts and video, with the visuals centralized because feed competition includes ads and AI content [mc7nK3PYgjw].
