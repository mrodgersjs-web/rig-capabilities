# Compensation and leveling: the tier model and dated numbers

Purpose: give a market model for front-end compensation (tier by level), leveling definitions by
scope, and the negotiation method, with every number dated and hearsay flagged.

When to load: any salary, band, level, offer, or negotiation question; any time someone quotes a
single number as "the market".

## Laws for this sub-topic

- Model a software market as three compensation spikes by company tier rather than as one
  average salary. CLAIM(1kO54TT2cbs@01:39) single-source.
- Classify tier 1 as small local companies: below-average pay but simpler interviews and better
  work-life balance, a reasonable place to start a career. CLAIM(1kO54TT2cbs@02:12) single-source.
- Classify tier 2 as cross-regional or international hirers (Deutsche Bank, JetBrains, Revolut,
  Toptal named) that pay high-average market salary. CLAIM(1kO54TT2cbs@02:43)
  CLAIM(1kO54TT2cbs@03:03) single-source.
- Classify tier 3 as the industry-leading large corporations (FAANG/MAANG), where the headline
  numbers live. CLAIM(1kO54TT2cbs@03:10) single-source.
- Define levels by scope, not years alone (junior needs mentoring; middle 2-5 years ships
  complex features independently; senior 5+ years leads a feature or product, mentors, and holds
  an architecture role; staff leads products or multiple teams' direction and understands the
  business). CLAIM(1kO54TT2cbs@03:50) CLAIM(1kO54TT2cbs@04:34) consensus.
- London tier 1 bands (2022): junior 17-29K GBP, middle 27-55K, senior 55-80K, no real staff
  data point. CLAIM(1kO54TT2cbs@05:12) CLAIM(1kO54TT2cbs@05:53) single-source, dated 2022.
- London tier 2 bands (2022): junior 29-55K, middle 55-87K, senior 80-150K, staff 120-190K, about
